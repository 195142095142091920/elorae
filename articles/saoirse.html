“Mother of groves, weaver of wreaths, and maiden of flowers: let me be rooted in Your peace, woven by Your gentleness, and ever returned to the blooming path of all living things.”

Born to the clergy of Arleth in the Balamwood. Bound in spirit to the hawk Aoife. Ward of Vaerek Rathkin.

Saoirse’s entrance to the world came with the first flowers of the Balamwood’s spring, or so she had always been told. It was a detail the ovates of Heartroot Sanctuary interpreted as an omen, as if the season of one's birth could foretell how their fate would bloom. She was born to prominent druids within a veiled Arlethian order and raised with their devotion to bestial magic and the healing arts. From her earliest years, nature's gifts revealed themselves to her with accelerated clarity. Lineage and talent together made her path to her parents' place in the priesthood an unquestioned inheritance to all around her. She embraced it, for a time, but stillness in one course did not always come easy to her. As a child, her attention strayed from the forest’s heart to its edges, drawn to the thinning of trees and beyond.

The certainty of her path would finally shift with the arrival of Vaerek Rathkin.

An outsider with a shrouded past, Vaerek was the first stranger in her short life to grace their halls. The young ranger’s presence unsettled the community despite their Archdruid's invitation, but that did not stop Saoirse's parents from opening their home to him as he came to embrace their ways. He carried himself with lessons learned outside the grove and instincts that leaned too readily toward tooth and steel; though perhaps these features should have repelled her, they instead awakened a world of interest in her, and she sought him out with relentless fascination. What began as a child’s curiosity settled over time into a more enduring bond. As he carved out his place among her people, no longer merely a tolerated outsider but a protector in his own right, he became a kinship nearer to family than duty. When he was named her Wreath Warden, she could barely contain her joy.

Fostering her growing hunger for exploration, Vaerek and her parents brought her on expeditions to villages and less-protected wilds. There she witnessed the work of the other druids and their wardens, learning early that the world’s wounds would not remain distant. It was during one such journey that she found an abandoned hatchling in a destroyed nest. She carried the young bird home and gave her the name Aoife. Through a ceremonial rite, their spirits were bound as sisters, awareness intertwined as Aoife grew by her side into a mighty hawk.

Years passed as Saoirse cultivated her calling. The lessons from her warden took deeper root with each passing season. Every scouting mission with Vaerek quietly shaped her worldview, and each encounter pressed a simple truth deeper into her: hesitation is its own kind of harm. So when she alone saw signs of Shiokian infiltration appear near their border, she did not seek counsel as instructed. She acted.

What followed still resists clear memory, coming to her only in fragments: claws tearing into flesh and sight alike, the disorienting collapse of control, and her attempt at justice twisted into a brutal example for sacrifice. Her survival came as an intervention beyond her own strength, owed to her Archdruid's resurfacing of a long-buried power. Her soul was rescued from the Hunt, but her salvation came at a cost that lingered long after their wounds closed, a fracture not easily named.

As her role in Heartroot's circle grew closer, that fracture only deepened. Where she once felt her Goddess in every breath of wind and turn of leaves, only stillness remained. Prayer became a discipline rather than a comfort. She dared not speak of it aloud despite Vaerek’s concern or the way his protective watch hovered over her every step. An unspoken tension settled between them, and she could not bear to remain where it lingered.

Whispers of unrest from the walls of Fala began to carry rumors of Shiokian corruption. Given another chance to confront her past failures, she invoked a sanctioned departure through the Rite of Pilgrimage, a path out that would foster pride rather than further scrutiny from her peers. With Aoife, she left her family behind to follow the signs southward until she encountered a band of travelers. After helping them fend off thieves, she offered her healing to them in exchange for an escort to the capital. Somewhere farther than the reach of the Balamwood, amid creeping curses and uncertain roads, her journey would test what remained of her faith and determine what it might yet become. Either way, she would not return unchanged.
