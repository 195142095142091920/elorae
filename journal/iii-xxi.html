Act III, Chapter XXI

After the harrowing night in the ruined chapel of Astar and the difficult burial of the fallen hunters, the party found welcome respite in the Guildhall of the Scourgeslayers in the village of Greymoor. For the first time in many days, they slept in warm, clean beds beneath a roof adorned with countless sigils and talismans of Sar Sareen. No nightmares plagued them that night—perhaps a subtle blessing of the goddess of restoration watched over the hall.

Dawn brought gentle rain. Saoirse rose first and approached Ment, the guarded Mageslayer, who sat writing in his journal at a table. She offered a quiet “Good morning” and inquired about his thoughts. He spoke solemnly of recording the loss of their comrades, acknowledging the dangers they had long accepted. The rest of the party stirred soon after. Saoirse gently woke the others, including Galand and Telorin from the upper loft, and shared the discovery pinned to the door: a farewell letter from Wren.

She read the bard’s words aloud to the gathered group. Wren confessed that the whispers from his dreams had only grown louder since arriving in this new world, accompanied by darkness from his past. He believed himself a danger to his companions and had set out alone to master whatever afflicted him, hoping one day their paths might cross again. The party reacted with shock and sorrow. Vaerek in particular noted how inconsolable Wren had seemed after the shared dreams. Though some suggested tracking him down, others respected his need for solitude, likening it to a druid’s quiet walk. Prayers were offered that the Red Scourge would not claim him first and that he might find his way back.

With Wren’s departure weighing on them, conversation turned to their broader quest. Saoirse and others pressed Sir Brigh and Lady Jiatra for more on local lore. Brigh recounted the scriptures of Sar Sareen: how the goddess, leading the remnants of the fallen Dalamand monarchy, was blessed by a magnificent tree during the war against the Six Kings. Through this tree she drew strength, banished the tyrannical kings to the southern Ochre Depths, and ascended to divinity. In exchange, the tree itself was consumed by the kings’ corrupting magic, leaving the wasteland a place of doom; horrors, beasts, and forbidden ground no mortal should tread. The motif of the tree, blended with water and restoration, adorned the guildhall.

The revelation that the Godtree of legend lay within the cursed Ochre Depths sent ripples through the party. Vaerek and Telorin pressed on the dangers, while Galand and Rallice gauged the threat. Brigh and the hunters warned sternly that entering the Depths without an army was suicide. The Red Scourge itself was suspected to originate from that blighted place, possibly still influenced by the banished kings. Plans shifted: the group resolved to travel to Yollonde’s capital to seek an audience with Queen Primwyn, present their case, and potentially secure aid - or even bring their draconic benefactor into the fray.

Before departing, they met with Mayor Callenrose in his cozy hall. The mayor, a childhood friend of Brigh, listened gravely. Though skeptical of their wild claims, he honored Brigh’s trust and provided a formal letter of introduction bearing Greymoor’s seal. Pouches of Red Scourge eyes - grisly trophies - were exchanged as tokens of the hunters’ ongoing vigilance. Saoirse and Vaerek spent hours in the guildhall’s tomes, poring over scriptures. Saoirse found detailed accounts of the tree’s role in Sar Sareen’s ascension and the banishment; Vaerek sought (with limited success) specifics on the Ochre Depths’ monsters and curses - mostly dire warnings to stay away.

In a quiet moment, Saoirse used Filibeth’s enchanted leaf to reach the Glaurfeldt princess. The connection held despite the distance. She updated Filibeth on their progress, the suspected location of a Godtree in the Ochre Depths, and the dire warnings about the region. Filibeth promised to relay the information to Heldranc and the other Dragonlords, offering potential assistance. Saoirse also shared what they had learned of the Red Scourge’s possible ties to the Six Kings.

The letter was delivered by the mayor's boy of ten years, an aspiring Scourgehunter. He marveled at Galand’s mighty greathammer, which only the Glaurfeldt warrior could lift, and the newcomers' displays of magic. Saoirse and others offered gentle guidance, emphasizing the value of protecting one’s people in many ways. Brigh and Jiatra agreed to accompany the party toward the capital as guides, representatives of their faith, and protectors, while Krim and Ment remained to safeguard Greymoor.

As the rain continued, the party prepared to depart at dawn for the multi-week journey to the capital. Skirmishes with the Red Scourge along the way were expected; Vaerek intended to test his draconic vigor further against the afflicted. With a letter from the mayor, the blessing of Sar Sareen’s faithful, and a tenuous link to their northern patrons, they set forth once more, hopeful that this path would lead not only to Darcdressil but to a way to stem the Red Scourge itself.
