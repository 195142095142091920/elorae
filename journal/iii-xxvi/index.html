Act III, Chapter XXVI

Everyone gathered in the common chamber in the evening. Vaerek entered with Saoirse, a blindfold across his eyes, yet he moved with a new certainty. Hearing and smell had been drawn tight as a dragon’s; he knew the ripple of the pool, the hush of whispered talk, the approaching steps in the hall.

He pulled the cloth down. Where his eyes should have been burned two fiery orbs.

Heldranc had given him draconic sight - the way the Kindred see in their true forms. The world came to him in shades of flame: outlines, frames, the twinkling cores of souls. Most were ordinary, shifting like stars. Galand’s was different - a condensed, ordered radiance of white-gold, contained rather than crackling. Filibeth’s light seemed to follow the shape of her whole being. Vaerek lingered on them a moment, then covered his eyes again. He would wear the blindfold now, and among strangers. The orbs were too much to show the world.

Filibeth was glad that he was in fighting shape once more.

Morning found Telorin already in the forge, shirtless, goggles on. Saoirse arrived with her spellbook. Together they finished a wand: Telorin completed the last of the rune, and she channeled a Circle of Protection spell into the wood in Wildword. When she tested it upon him, a wave of Arleth’s calm settled over him - a peace so deep it felt like sedation. He understood, for a moment, why she had given her life to that goddess.

Galand’s tattoo would wait for private work: great wing-shapes across his back, an ambitious fusion of ink and a rechargeable arcane effect.

At midday the Lords convened in the throne room. Tension still hung between Montmorian and Aszurithice. She spoke of the Underrealm.

Deep beneath the Winterlands the caverns opened onto a frozen underground sea. Souls walked the ice and rose like vapor - one mind, one purpose, one hivemind. In service to Celurna, she had frozen them and wrung the same answers from every one. They served Ito Gangara: the Hungerer, Queen of the Core, Queen of the Void. She dwelt beneath the ice deeper still. So long as she remained there, the hive would rise and kill and grow.

South of that sea lay the Silver Forest, a place of ensorcelled silver-leaved trees and dark shadows. Beyond it, a white desert and a kingdom she had not entered. Saoirse’s eyes widened. Wren had dreamed of silver trees. Filibeth remembered a Spectre King her father had once meant to invade.

Heldranc set the course. The realm below belonged to such demons. They must first strengthen the realm above. Cadarinost was next: a proud, reclusive brother who had made himself a hidden archive in the sands, and who had spoken of something called the Revenant Host.

Nyralshirad would accompany them. Then, after debate, Pethengorom as well i his knowledge of souls and his affinity for sand better suited than Nyralshirad’s impatience to fight.

Saoirse asked the Lords how they wished to be seen. She spoke of Vaerek’s fire that could cleanse as well as scour, of Yollonde’s queen who would not ally with her ancestors’ oppressors, of Darkdressil already named Terror of the Depths. She claimed that if the dragons returned only as flame, the world would be as the K'bas, and that they needed to prove they were not Mano.

To this Montmorian and Nyralshirad took offense. The Dragonlord of the Woodland cared little for mortal perception. He explained that demons and their thralls were the enemy, those who should be killed by any means. Pethengorom offered to travel in his place. Montmorian, kingly and raw beneath the surface, granted that they would discern the souls before they struck - and that the K'bas would never happen again. In a sharp and assertive tone, he spoke that he would not be spoken to as a servant, reminding Saoirse and the rest that, though they were appreciated, they were not equals. This was a marked change in demeanor from what the servants had grown accustomed to. Time was running out and a life had been lost.

They stepped through Pethengorom’s portal into Uma Sura. It was raining.

Thunder rolled without pause. Lightning walked the wet dunes, leaving blackened craters and glass in the sand. Pethengorom had never read of rain in these lands. He raised a crystal bubble from his staff and the storm sheeted off it while they trudged through the muck toward old white-sandstone ruins.

This was Akath, the southern reaches. North lay the true white sands, and somewhere among them, they hoped, Cadarinost.

They took shelter under weathered stone as lightning and thunder held the sky, and the next day’s journey waited.
