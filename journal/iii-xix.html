Act III, Chapter XIX

The party pressed onward along the muddy roads of Syr Sable, accompanied by the wary Hunters of Greymoor. Rain fell in fitful bursts, soaking the endless fields and hills that stretched toward distant mountains. The hunters rode in loose formation, some ahead and others circling back, their vigilance never wavering as they watched for any sign of the dreaded Red Scourge. They were always alert, always circling, ensuring the safety of the group in this dangerous land.

After three quiet nights of travel - with Wren offering no songs for the group and the campfires burning low and subdued - the fourth day brought them to evening. As the party settled around the fire, Vaerek suggested that he and Wren take the first watch. Wren agreed with a wry smile, remarking that he had always been something of a night owl.

As the others drifted off to sleep, Vaerek’s tone grew serious. He asked Wren if he was alright, recalling the strange trance that had overtaken him a few nights earlier while playing a song. Wren had seemed lost, his eyes unfocused, as if he had slipped away into some deep, fearful absence. Wren tried to brush it off as nothing more than a passionate performance, the kind of concentration that came naturally to him when playing. But Vaerek was not convinced. He pressed gently, noting that Wren rarely spoke of his past - only passing mentions, never a full story. It felt like quiet omission rather than deliberate avoidance. Vaerek made it clear he was not trying to force confessions or twist arms; he simply wanted to know his companion was safe. The conversation ended with mutual assurances of friendship and support, though an undercurrent of mystery lingered in the firelight.

The morning came, and the party returned to the road. They traveled all day until evening, when the rains returned once more. As twilight settled over the land, the first structure they had seen in four days appeared in the distance - the ruins of an old chapel nestled near trees off the road. The hunters announced they would rest there for the night. It was an ancient chapel of Astar, the dead god of twilight. The group took shelter inside, finding dry corners amid damaged pews and leaking roofs. As they settled in, conversation turned to the nature of gods and mortality. Brigh, the burly paladin of Sar Sareen, explained that gods rely on their worshippers. In his words, without hearts bent in reverence, a god loses power and has no pulse in the mortal world. Saoirse added her own perspective, describing divinity as a relationship between souls rather than pure immortal power. Vaerek and others debated the implications, wondering how gods could truly die and whether the same rules applied to demons or other entities.

As the night deepened, Saoirse and Ment conversed near the doorway, both taking first watch. Saoirse and Ment spoke quietly near the doorway, their voices low against the patter of rain. Ment asked about her connection to Aoife, her hawk companion. Saoirse explained that while she could not fully control the bird’s will, she could share sight with her through an Arlethian ritual. Ment observed that she did not carry the look of a northerner. Saoirse replied that her parents were not from the north and that her upbringing as a druid had been different. She mentioned her mother was a gifted shapeshifter who could take the form of ravens. Ment inquired what such a transformation felt like and looked like. Saoirse described willing shapeshifts as gentle and seamless, while forced ones were gruesome and painful.

The philosophical discussion was shattered by sudden horror. Three of the hunters - Talke, Tenn, and Leonis - rose with glowing red eyes, consumed by the Red Scourge. They attacked without hesitation, their movements unnaturally strong and devoid of pain. Chaos erupted in the tight confines of the ruined chapel. Jiatra shrieked as one loomed over her. Brigh dragged her to safety with grim determination. Krim and Ment sprang into action with deadly precision. The party leapt to fight alongside them.

Galand’s eyes began to glow with an eerie divine light as battle instincts took hold. He moved with fluid, unnatural grace, his greathammer striking with devastating force. Saoirse sustained and expanded her blessing, bathing the group in golden radiance, and attempted to dispel the curse afflicting the hunters. Vaerek dodged attacks from the enthralled and used his own blood in an experimental healing attempt. Telorin maneuvered skillfully, striking from the flanks with his rapier. Wren and Perstrin cast offensive sorcery as the skirmish raged.

The corrupted hunters fought with terrifying resilience, but they were eventually brought down through coordinated effort and holy power. Jiatra called a pillar of Sareen's holy light upon Talke. Vaerek, in anger, grabbed the afflicted hunter Tenn and spoke a warning to whatever might be watching through his red eyes, before unleashing Heldranc's dragonfire upon him. The chapel fell silent once more, the air thick with the metallic scent of blood and the weight of sudden loss. The surviving hunters stared in shock and grief at the companions they had just been forced to kill. Galand’s glowing eyes lingered for a moment longer before slowly fading. The party stood amid the ruins, the weight of the night’s violence hanging heavy in the twilight air of Astar’s chapel.
