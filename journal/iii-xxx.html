Act III, Chapter XXX

The green portal left nothing behind. Perstrin’s tattoos burned as he stared at the empty sand where the dragonslayer had stood, and he told Telorin the magic had been pure, cleaner than anything Pethengorom had ever woven, gone in five seconds, with no arcane trace.

Montmorian went to his twin on all fours. He nudged Cadarinost’s snout the way a beast tries to wake one who won’t, and a whimper rose from him, the largest of the Dragons. Then he screamed. The sound dropped Vaerek to his knees with blood at his ears. Wings kicked white sand into the twin suns, when Montmorian circled once and flew.

Vaerek climbed the corpse. There was no slayer’s blood to take a scent from, only the glaive’s work: deep stabs at the nape, cuts too clean for mundane steel, and a faint char as if the scales had been softened before the killing stroke. Saoirse found no lingering magic on the wounds. Telorin and Perstrin focused their attention on the magic used. With his mimicry, Perstrin opened an oval of his own, Nathalor visible on the far side, so Telorin could watch the working while it lasted. The style matched the future-Perstrin they had met in the Glaurfeldt’s past, though whether that meant the slayer came from then, or now, they could not say.

Saoirse laid peaceful rest between Cadarinost’s scales, and white light settled into the wounds.

Heldranc found his vessel. Vaerek’s eyes went red-orange, and his gait and voice were no longer his. The dragon-lord clutched Cadarinost’s head and spoke unintelligible Draconic, then demanded the slayer’s description. The witnesses spoke their account: dark armor, a black cloak, a pointed helm, and a glaive sparking green. Terror took the borrowed face. They were all dead, he said — thousands of years dead. The Dragonslayers of Raim.

A scream in the distance broke the sorrow. Vaerek, possessed, snatched Valeburn and ran. Saoirse followed her warden’s body; the rest of the group came after, through cream-white dunes, until they found the second body. Pethengorom still breathed. Blood poured from his mouth onto the Celestial Sands and one claw was gone. Montmorian stood over him with a wing lowered.

Saoirse emptied her staff of its restorative power. Perstrin copied what he could. Telorin took the leaf into his shaking hands; the raw infinite power nearly unmade him before Filibeth answered and asked whether she should bring them back.

They gathered and Montmorian became a man again, tears of rage in his eyes. Warmth took them to the Athag’ir Godtree. Filibeth’s pink-white flood closed sinew and crystal while Vaerek’s scaly triage invigorated the wounded dragon. Pethengorom resumed human shape, right hand missing, blood at the mouth, eyes open.

The Weaver spoke. A knight, he said. Magically silent. Pain in the back, then again. He had swatted, felt a blast of power, seen blood fall from the sky, and hardened his scales to gemstone. The dragonslayer had left him to bleed out. Cadarinost was a corpse. The word hit him like a second wound.

Perstrin and Telorin anchored a portal to the sanctuary and they carried the Weaver through. Heldranc waited at the foot of the throne — the ancient face showed fear. Fifteen stewards laid Pethengorom on cushions. Aszurithice cried that he lived. Nyralshirad stood silent. Heldranc asked two questions: whether the cloak had been black, and whether the helm had a spike. The group confirmed these truths once more.

It was a nightmare returned, he said. They were describing the armor of one man in particular. The ebon raiment of the Lord High Dragonslayer of the Empire of Raim, the commander by whose order dragonkind was exterminated. His true name was lost. Among dragons he was known only as Eig, the draconic word for fear.

Galand then spoke of the pit in the caverns. In the dark he had seen Vorn on a green plain, channeling the night that sent forth the Revenants, and he had answered with the light within him — an infinitesimal vestige of Caurobor, the Light Soul, the same light that lived in the Alabaster Blade.

The slayer was not of Vorn. The host was. They were fallen of Fala, displaced, wearing Montmorian’s crest, corrupted by the whispers of the Dark and answering the Beckoning Bell of Enthar. The same whispers that had already reached for Wren.

Pethengorom would rest, then reach for Darcdressil and Thalancord. Night fell on Nathalor. Telorin went to the forge with a phantasmal servant. In the aviary that opened on winter sky, Vaerek taught the air Heldranc’s fury, producing a cone of sonic force that threw gravel into the dark night. Galand joined with Rallice’s sword. As allies, they dueled, as they once did upon their first meeting. Rock cracked and blood marked the stone — two men exhibiting their strength and ever-growing might, readying themselves for the certain wars to come.
