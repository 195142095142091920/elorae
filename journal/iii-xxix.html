Act III, Chapter XXIX

The fight ran both sides of the dark. Magical night poured from a pit that dropped without bottom. Revenants climbed it like a tide. Montmorian’s Alabaster Blade threw white light as he cut them down and kicked them back into the drop. Vaerek flew the ledge, Valeburn burning as it struck, and called warning as more souls rose from below. He dropped to a wounded Phantom and dragged her back from the edge.

Galand’s eyes went pale gold. Wings of pure light unfurled from his back — Telorin’s work made flesh — and the cavern flashed. He dove through the wall of dark, hammer first, then lightning-swapped to Rallice’s greatsword. He rose again trailing light like a fading star.

Telorin flanked and put his rapier through one revenant; electricity leapt to the next and both fell. Then he cast Sanguine Mist into the press. The undead drank the void and stood taller. He dropped the spell the moment he understood. Saoirse lashed vitality through the dark, then blessed all allies. Six bodies moved with sudden surety — Arleth’s peace in the step. She called them around the pit to the far fight, where Phantoms were already dying.

Galand flew into the void alone. What he saw there he kept. He came back out, hammer sparking, and the last of the near host broke. Darkness receded. Torchlight returned. The pit was empty of souls, as if the night had never been.

Montmorian stood among the fallen in Fala plate and stared until his eyes glazed. He billowed his cloak. Vaerek healed who he could. To the surviving Phantoms he said they had fought well, and to burn the bodies with respect — these had been people taken. Montmorian did not look back.

They walked a cavern of massacre. Void had been here, killed everyone, and left no trace. The tunnel climbed toward heat and morning. They stepped out onto the Celestial Sands. White alabaster dunes. Twin suns. No rain. No thunder.

A dragon cried across the sky. It was not Pethengorom. Sand-colored, desert-scaled, it came screeching. Montmorian’s mouth found a smirk — then Vaerek saw the truth. The dragon’s soul was dim, dimmer even than the host. Paired with it rode a soul as bright as a lord’s.

The flight failed. Wings folded. The beast tumbled and struck the dunes a thousand feet out. Sand slammed the party. When the dust settled, Cadarinost lay dying. An armored man stood on the corpse, cape billowing, glaive crackling green. The magic pulled from the wound. Vaerek watched the dragon’s soul climb into the slayer. Cadarinost’s head fell.

Montmorian whispered Brother. Then he screamed it. Vaerek felt the loss in the old bond with dragonkind; steam rose from his eyes instead of tears.

Montmorian ran. Light and fire burst as he took his true form and landed on the dunes, throat filling with flame. A green oval opened behind the slayer. He stepped back through it. The portal closed. He was gone.
