Act III, Chapter XLVI

The storm thickened with thunder as the cursed rose again. Kojin cut one down and called for Hanto. Saoirse flew to Vaerek, who still held Filibeth, and cleared her mind; the priestess’s eyes rolled back and she slipped unconscious. He set her on a thick branch under Yuo’s watch, then returned to the field where soul-sight showed lingering cores in most of the dead save those Hanto had already claimed.

Telorin drove a spellstrike into a ghoul beside the wall of water. The thunderstorm answered with doubled lightning that arced through the barrier and flash-steamed the surrounding shallows. Silar pressed the bloated zombie with stormburst and spiritual weapon while Hanto claimed more souls. Galand, still touched by the laughter, stood unfazed as the smiling ghouls struck him. The bloated one, its eyes flaring under the tree’s tendrils, reached up and swatted Perstrin from the air.

Baiji’s blessing widened as she and the others finished the last of the smaller cursed. Yuo put a heavy shot into the big one. Vaerek dropped to heal Perstrin and shouted that they must burn the face from the tree. Wolves answered—rotting, half-skeletal things. Telorin slowed time around the trunk. Silar summoned a primal dragon in the likeness of Thalencord that rose from reddish mist and breathed fire across the face.

Filibeth stirred on her branch, looked at the grimacing visage, and knew it for Olesh; she said they must cleanse it as Thalencord once had. Vaerek centered himself and called on Heldranc. The Dragon Lord answered, took the body, and turned the wings to obsidian black. He named the face an old enemy and breathed fire laced with golden sparks. Tendrils reached for him and failed. The company pressed the wolves and the tree together. Perstrin called a ray of sunlight that briefly parted the clouds and seared the bark.

Saoirse gave Filibeth the leaf; the priestess began to commune with it while Vaerek and Heldranc, sharing the body more cleanly, hurled Valeburn into one of the face’s eyes. A guttural voice answered that it could not die. The wall of water fell. Filibeth’s light intensified and restrained the tendrils. She opened her eyes and said that he was here. A shriek answered from the storm. Golden light filled the sky.

Thalencord crested the trees, circled, and landed upon the god-tree itself. He drove his claws into the wood and pushed the fire deeper into the face until the scream ended and the visage was gone. Vaerek’s vigor returned. He flew into the remaining flame with Heldranc and drove Valeburn into the mouth while calling the Lord’s fury. The company cheered. The bodies lay still, their laughter ended and their eyes dark. The fire burned only the corruption, leaving an ordinary tree.

Act III, Chapter XLV

The rain continued as Saoirse opened a circle of healing that restored the living and forced the laughter back from the nearest of the cursed. Filibeth stayed near the center of the company, watching every direction. Vaerek, hearing that Saoirse had been hurt, cut one of the ghouls in half with a pillar of flame and moved to reach her, though the attempt to heal her failed in his haste. He told the others to keep watch on Filibeth while Telorin and Silar pressed the remaining undead, and Yuo put a heavy arrow into one from a branch above.

Under the water a new sound rose, and a massively bloated, waterlogged zombie with bright orange eyes and a wide smile rose from the shallows. Perstrin slowed it from the air. Saoirse lashed another of the cursed and spoke of the things that did not belong in Arleth’s garden. Filibeth remained fixed on the face in the tree. Through his sight Vaerek saw the tendrils of its core reaching toward her, and she asked why, already dazed.

Vaerek sealed the last of his own wounds, called his wings, and took Filibeth into the air with him, shaking her and telling her to snap out of it and cleanse the tree. She looked at him through wet hair and asked why again. Telorin pinned the bloated one through the leg with a rising spike of metal. Galand erupted with laughter that filled the field while his own wings flaring as he rose to meet the bloated zombie’s eyes. The smaller cursed that had fallen began to rise again - missing limbs, one of them only a torso - and their laughter came all at once.

Baiji said they needed to leave or to burn the tree. Galand forced a moment of clarity and remained able to act, though the laughter still sat on him. Vaerek shifted Filibeth to one arm, hurled Valeburn into the bloated zombie so that it burst into flame and returned to his hand, and called to Yuo to ready a place in the tree. Telorin tripped the bloated one with sorcery. The face in the bark lost its smile and became a grimace; the laughter turned darker.

Saoirse judged the field and called that the undead would not stop rising until the tree was cleansed. Filibeth, still not fully present, said she thought she should see the face. Vaerek answered with Heldranc’s Presence, trying to drive out whatever held her. She spoke of dragons and of the tree, and asked why his eyes were as they were. He brought her toward Saoirse and told her to get the leaf so that Filibeth could be cleansed first.

Telorin dropped in green flame among a cluster of the cursed and called fiery wrath across them. More of the ghouls tried the wall of water. Galand put his head down and forced the tendrils that had been reaching into him to cut off; through soul sight Vaerek watched the knight’s soul harden into a denser core edged with a barrier. The face in the tree remained, grimacing, and the laughter had not fully left the field. Filibeth was still not fully present. The cursed kept rising. The company held the ground they had taken, but the tree had not been cleansed.

Act III, Chapter XLIV

They followed Aoife’s lead toward the withered tree and found it standing in a shallow lake, its roots gripping a gnarled bank at the water’s edge. Filibeth knelt by the lakeside to commune with it and attempt a purification. Saoirse joined her. The ground directly beneath Saoirse cleared, but the rest of the corruption would take far longer time than they had. Filibeth sank deeper into the working until she entered a trance.

Vaerek and Galand kept watch. Through his draconic sight Vaerek saw the tree’s core reaching outward in tendrils, and he warned the others to stay back. Galand shook Filibeth to wake her and was struck by a rush of flashing, dreamlike images. She fell onto her back and came to herself. Laughter rose in the distance. It unnerved Hanto and Kojin; they drew their blades and called the company to fight. The cursed of Olesh were upon them. The sky darkened and rain came quickly. On the tree a contorted humanoid face of twisted wood appeared, its eyes glowing red-orange.

The ghouls advanced through the muddy shallows. Hanto moved to meet them and told Baiji to shout if anything came on her side. She loosed an arrow tipped with bluish-white light. One of the things struck Hanto; he held. Galand’s first strikes failed to land. Silar rose on a swirl of embers and took the air, while Yuo watched from a heavy branch above. Kojin closed with the heavy sword and cut one of the undead down. Saoirse raised a wall of water from the river, catching several of the ghouls inside it and holding the rest at a distance. Vaerek drove Valeburn through the barrier in a short stab; steam rose where the blade met the water, though the blows went wide.

Telorin looked past the wall. The face in the bark remained, its eyes darting and the wood twisting. He cast forth a cloud of rusted air into the rear of the pack, then stepped back. Hanto struck again. Baiji fell back toward Kojin and put arrows into the things pressing him. Some of the ghouls forced their way through the water and came around Galand; one left Vaerek drained, though he shook the worst of it off.

Silar hovered above the clump and called for the others to clear away. Galand’s next efforts faltered. The company began sharing the labor of the remnants: Hanto’s blade, Silar’s fire, Baiji’s bow, Kojin’s sword. Saoirse cast a mercy meant to blunt the undead’s blows against the living, then moved clear. Baiji put arrows into the ghouls flanking Galand, one of them true. Telorin stepped behind one that had reached Galand, his staff becoming a rapier, and drove a spellstrike home that left the creature bleeding. Silar answered with fire from above. Most endured it; three took the burn and kept coming, smoldering and still laughing. A current from Saoirse swept one back toward the far side. Galand landed a heavy blow at last. Yuo loosed from the branch. Perstrin tested an icicle against the one Yuo had marked.

The face had not left the tree. The ghouls that remained were fewer, but they were not finished, and Saoirse had taken hard hits. Vaerek was already turning toward her as the rain continued and the laughter had not yet gone silent.

Act III, Chapter XLIII

The hearth still burned behind Master Yudai, and Heldranc looked through Vaerek into the flame with Valeburn lowered at his side. Saoirse called rain into the room; it drowned the pit fire, yet the hearth climbed higher, and she set the blindfold over Vaerek’s eyes while naming the power that held him as belonging to Izil. Hanto judged the old man free of Olesh and drew his sword, whose green enchantment yearned toward the soul already reaching for them.

Vaerek returned only partway to himself, moving enfeebled, while the heat hurt him in a way fire almost never did. Saoirse raised a wall of water across the hearth and spoke her druidic chant, and the flame fought before falling back to embers. Yudai rose then, speaking of bones and warm ash, and Hanto took his head in a single stroke. Galand watched the trail of the soul enter the blade. Vaerek drove Valeburn into what remained, and Heldranc’s deeper voice rose under his own as the hearth blew apart.

Kojin found the blood clean of 'venom'. Saoirse closed the old man’s eyes and named what they had faced: Izil, Lord of Flame, whom she and Telorin had met once in Greenbank, a power that took those who stared too long into his fire. Vaerek chose to let Heldranc stay. The lord spoke through him, confirming that the enthrallment had broken, and that Izil was a known ashen lord from beyond the Silent City. He welcomed the Thalane Remnants into Nathalor upon the completion of their quest. They spoke of the storms, of Raith, and of the old binding between Shiok and consuming flame, though Heldranc would not choose their road for them.

They settled on the Godtree. If Thalancord lived he might be waiting there, and Filibeth could begin to reclaim it; the remnants would accompany them. Before he withdrew, Heldranc answered that Izil’s flames were not born of dragons and would indeed harm them.

Vaerek set Yudai’s body in the cracked hearth and breathed dragonfire over it until only ash remained. They left at dawn Ai Ishi. Aoife led them north along the mountain road; a week passed with no word of Thalancord, and at the edge of Kaiden they crossed into Ai Ishi, where the curse had already touched the country. A wood rose ahead of them, and they turned northeast off the road into land that grew steadily lusher.

They could not find the tree. Hanto remembered that in the old days the Emperor’s Guard had entered these woods alone while the rest waited on the road, and it seemed the place was concealed as the K'bas had been. Saoirse called Filibeth through the leaf. Green mist gathered, and the princess stepped into the open light, easing the barrier of tongues so that each heard the others clearly.

She touched the river and then the leaf, and a spot of brown appeared where the water met it. Saoirse felt rot in the stream, the same signature she had known in Athag'ir, and named Horlg, divine of plague and blight. If the water could wither a leaf of a god-tree, the tree itself might already be dying. Aoife went up once more and found a gnarled, twisted tree standing dead on a riverside. They turned toward it, knowing Filibeth would need them if resistance waited there.

Act III, Chapter XLII

They set off out of Kuroishi toward an old outpost of Kaiden, spending the translation scrolls an hour at a time so that Hanto spoke and Saoirse carried his words. From the heights, the country lay open, fair, and visibly battered. Aoife flew high. The land fell sheer to the south and more gently to the north, where the rivers ran down, and Saoirse judged that a basin would lie that way. The birds had sung of a great pink tree through such a country. She believed she knew the likely direction of this land's Godtree.

Hanto knew it. It stood in the Emperor’s Wood, in Shune’s Basin, where the Lord of Kaiden had gone once a year. Only noble blood was meant to look upon the leaves. Saoirse explained that In other lands, trees of this kind had been threatened, and the Dragonlords had tried to defend them. Shune, as he told it, was a lady who had united her people against an old oppression. What Kaiden remembered was the endurance. Hanto would die with a sword in his hand.

The Remnants had been made the same way. After the fall of the empire, Hanto met Yuo, who nearly shot him, because other Kaiden soldiers had already gone mad. They found Silar, whose company had died, and in a village of survivors found Kojin, a farmer gifted with a blade, and his wife Baiji. Thalancord took them in, sent them south to gather forces, and vanished. That was eight or nine months past. His gold had been restorative: courage washing a field as light, and the madness seeming to quiet under it.

Fort Red Eve, high in the mountains of Kaiden.

That night Vaerek told Silar the rest. He explained again to the scholar that he was Heldranc’s vessel, given eyes and baptized in flame. Silar, worn thin by the work of putting innocents to the sword, asked to join the Kindred when this was done, and brightened further at the thought Pethengorom, a Dragon willing to share the deep knowledge he pursued.

The fort stood in the wind, covered in a dusting of snow. They knocked. Aoife saw no movement for a time. Soon, the great portcullis opened upon one old man with a cane. He was called Master Yudai - alone for two months, hoping stone and height would let him finish his days in quiet. A nearby village had gone to the madness, laughing, eyes orange and bleeding. His sons were long dead. He hoped to be met by Vana, goddess of death. He had not seen Thalancord, and he spoke as if that plight had already ended in disaster. He invited them in.

As they talked around a pit of flame and ash, the fire climbed. Vaerek lifted his blindfold. His soul was tattered. Tendrils of the soul extended from the core of the elder's body and yearned toward the others. Saoirse set a hand upon his head. The magic was beyond their rank, akin to what had possessed Nyralshirad, yet unalike its composition. While the Dark was cold, this was hot as a hand over a campfire. Her cleansing rebounded. She fell back into Vaerek, swearing she had been burned, and found no mark.

The pull left the man and took the room. Saoirse gazed upon the pit. Vaerek was held by the soul. Galand felt the same enamoration in the flame. Thunder cracked outside. The old man said they would be safe here, and that he joined them.

Master Yudai sits, enthralled by the flame of Izil.


Saoirse knew the flame. She had met it at the start of the journey, beside that candlemaker who walked into the desert and died. She put a gust through the pit and brought down a flash of rain indoors. The firepit died. The hearth behind them, rose higher, and Aoife shrieked at the coming storm. Vaerek drew Valeburn and set the edge at the elders throat. The old man muttered words and beckoned them to continue - the flame would devour his bones.

Vaerek's eyes changed, and the voice, and Heldranc looked at a wet room and a hearth still raging. Saoirse named the presence, this was Izil. Heldranc lowered the sword, turned to the flame, and gazed deeply upon the flame.

Act III, Chapter XLI

Five remained of Thalane’s order, and that truth sat with them at the fire while the others watched from the rocks, unable to understand a word.

Silar, who had followed rumor of dragons across the sea, asked how long they had sought Thalancord. Only the hours Kojin had already seen, Vaerek told him, though the search for the Drake’s brothers and sister had gone on far longer. He named himself Rathkin, a son of a Shasirian mother. Silar took his hand. He was of Shasir as well. Neither had met another Sethian in a long time. He spoke aside to Kojin, who laughed, and called it fate: they had not meant to be at Redroot at all. The Interval had begun, and Thalancord was missing.

The five around the fire were all that was left. Their mentor and most of their companions had been taken by the curse. Vaerek named his companions. Silar noted the blindfold and was told there were other senses than sight.

Silar had come here when he learned that a power was fighting Olesh’s venom and that the power might be draconic. He had met Thalancord, as the order all had. The charge given down from Thalane was to find a cure if one existed, and if not to cut the afflicted down and give the bodies to flame. They had come to Redroot on a thin hope, found the village already fallen, and done what the duty required.

An interval, Silar said, was the turning of the cycle. Olesh had been slain three times and had risen three times. The old way was to gather militias without letting the curse pool, find where the madness gathered, and threaten it until Olesh came to restore it, and then to kill him. After great storms the land itself announced his return — thunders, rains, winds. Animals went frantic and rabid, forest creatures and household pets turned. Telorin said each country seemed to wear a different face of the same threat, and that something godlike sat behind it. He named Mintas Fae of Hesk, Vehem Adahr of the Samafi, and Vorn of the lands below. Silar poured wine called Tears of the Rains and said that here, at least, the enemy had a name and a season. After the last battles Thalancord had simply vanished. No body. The sky darkened for weeks; when the rains broke and the woods went loud, Olesh was returned.

In the morning they would walk a country they had not seen in two years, asking for Thalancord at every door: Kuroishi and its smith at its Ash-Forge, Fort Red Eve, a mountain shrine, Minashi, a Tea Village, and last Kireji in the valley.

They would travel together. Telorin and Perstrin set to work on scrolls, for translation. Vaerek asked what Silar knew of the Vigors. Silar called fire into his palm and said he served the first wardens of the world. Vaerek said he used them too, though not as a sorcerer, and walked him from the fire to tell him how that had come to be.

Morning came clear. Hanto fetched an odachi and met Galand in the clearing. The blade took on a green light. Galand’s eyes went gold, and they struck, steel against steel. Until Galand pulled back, and he knew what occurred — his soul pulled thin, just as Eig’s glaive had pulled. Hanto called his victory chance. The blade was Kuranata, Blade of the Damura, bound to cut spirit as well as flesh, for blood alone did not always finish Olesh’s work. Silar did not think the enchantment came from Raim. Thalane himself had come from the west, and that was all he knew.

Vaerek and Saoirse closed the wounds. Then they spoke the news they had spared the night before. A slayer of Raim walked. His name was Eig. He had killed a brother of Thalancord and maimed another, and two more lords had been lost to cataclysm besides. The remnant heard it as a cold thing. Silar had already heard of disaster in his homeland, and of a dragonlord taken there. Whether that force served Olesh, no one could say.

The scrolls opened speech between the companies. Galand embraced Hanto, named himself a vestige of the Light Soul, and unfurled wings. Kojin asked how the slayer fought. If Eig was already in this country, Thalancord would not survive.

They took the high road of Kaiden, where the land was still fair and the trees showed the last storm. Saoirse left the column with Aoifa, called birds down, and asked about the weather. The storm was worse than others, they told her, longer and louder. Kojin said the same: two weeks, sometimes three, then a silence like an ending.

Kuroishi sat on a high river. Folk bowed to Hanto and stared at the rest. Children asked Galand whether a dragon was like the Nomura-beasts from the ridges, seven-legged and three-headed. Telorin found the Ash-Forge, a heart of flame that could take metal ordinary fire could not, and watched a smith fold mundane metal.

Perstrin looked at the dust in the street and longed for his past life. They spread through the village to ask again. Saoirse kept one question for the birds: whether their migrations still sang of a great pink tree.

Act III, Chapter XL

The common chamber was quiet when Galand and Vaerek entered, exhausted. Filibeth was there, awaiting Galand’s return. Pethengorom recovered in his chamber. Saoirse was asleep and awoke to their entering the central pool. Cadarinost’s death sat foremost in Vaerek’s mind; Heldranc’s anguish and Montmorian’s grief still moved in him.

The next day, Vaerek went to Montmorian in his sanctum, who was in silent grief. He spoke his condolence for the twin. They had hatched together and never been far apart, and Montmorian had watched him die with nothing in his power to stop it. Wars between kingdoms at least declared themselves. This one did not. They named Eig and named the dark and still did not know what either wanted. This ignorance had already taken Rallice in the Winterlands and now nearly two more in the sands. They could not carry that same blindness into the Farland.

Vaerek asked that the dragonlords remain behind. If a slayer hunted their kind, the last of them should not walk the road. Montmorian accepted the warning, agreed that they could not delay, and asked to be left.

Telorin tended his workshop in the days that followed. Rallice’s absence made the work quieter than it should have been. Over the next couple of days, he took Galand’s maul and bound into it stronger runes. Saoirse came while he worked and asked what the leaf had been like in his hands. He explained that he had begun to lose himself in it before Filibeth reached him. She also asked whether they could set down on paper what she had done to cleanse herself of curses, against the chance that the Scourge had taken Darcdressil.

On the third day they gathered in the throne room. Heldranc would not watch fear take another of his children. Two roads remained: the Depths, for Darcdressil, or Far Nybei, for Thalancord. Pethengorom stood hooded at the side, well enough now to speak, one forearm gone. He spoke that the Current over Nybei was wrong. Souls there burned too brightly — not dim as the Host, not bright as Filibeth or Galand, only disturbed. He would set them down near the one that still looked as a soul should. They might find Thalancord as they had found Cadarinost: by following his enemy.

Vaerek said again that the lords should not travel. Heldranc agreed and forbade the Lords from leaving the sanctuary. Pethengorom would remain and learn how Eig moved. Heldranc wished for he and Aszurithice to bring Cadarinost home from the sand and lay him in the tomb beneath Nathalor — but for now, this was too dangerous. Saoirse asked a place in the city also for the phantoms who had served Cadarinost; it was granted.

Perstrin stated he could now recall them back to the sanctuary, but he did not possess the power to read the Current as Pethengorom. Saoirse called Aoife. The green gate opened. They stepped through into autumn.

They came out at the edge of a village under trees that twisted as they rose. Woodsmoke hung in the air. A man stood over the dead and watched them arrive. Vaerek smelled blood and drew Valeburn. No one among them spoke the man’s tongue. Telorin used a scroll of tongues, and understood him alone.

He named himself Kojin En, a swordsman of the Remnants of Thalane. Redroot Village had fallen to the curse, and he and his group had cleared it. The dead were fresh but unnatural — burned, lidless, orange eyes, rotting while still warm. No magic remained on them. Telorin mentioned Thalancord. Kojin struck his chest at Thalancord’s name and said he sought him also. These, he told them, were victims of Olesh. They attempted speech with difficulty.

He offered his camp. They walked an hour into the countryside of somewhere in Kaiden, through mist and turning leaves. He named the others as they came into view: Baiji En, his wife; Hanto Han, a warrior of Kaiden; Silar Scorria, a dragon scholar; and Yuo Nanaga, an archer.

Speech failed them until Vaerek tried Draconic. Silar answered it. He was a scholar of dragons and knew the name they carried. Thalancord was known as the Sun Gold Drake. Thalane, the once-lord of their order, had been Thalancord’s teacher. From his name, Thalancord took his, and upheld the duty to defend Nybei against curse and terror. They were unsurprised that the Kindred had come from across the sea, and they were not afraid of what that meant.

Silar gestured them to the fire. If they sought the Sun Gold Drake, there was more to say.

Act III, Chapter XXX

The green portal left nothing behind. Perstrin’s tattoos burned as he stared at the empty sand where the dragonslayer had stood, and he told Telorin the magic had been pure, cleaner than anything Pethengorom had ever woven, gone in five seconds, with no arcane trace.

Montmorian went to his twin on all fours. He nudged Cadarinost’s snout the way a beast tries to wake one who won’t, and a whimper rose from him, the largest of the Dragons. Then he screamed. The sound dropped Vaerek to his knees with blood at his ears. Wings kicked white sand into the twin suns, when Montmorian circled once and flew.

Vaerek climbed the corpse. There was no slayer’s blood to take a scent from, only the glaive’s work: deep stabs at the nape, cuts too clean for mundane steel, and a faint char as if the scales had been softened before the killing stroke. Saoirse found no lingering magic on the wounds. Telorin and Perstrin focused their attention on the magic used. With his mimicry, Perstrin opened an oval of his own, Nathalor visible on the far side, so Telorin could watch the working while it lasted. The style matched the future-Perstrin they had met in the Glaurfeldt’s past, though whether that meant the slayer came from then, or now, they could not say.

Saoirse laid peaceful rest between Cadarinost’s scales, and white light settled into the wounds.

Heldranc found his vessel. Vaerek’s eyes went red-orange, and his gait and voice were no longer his. The dragon-lord clutched Cadarinost’s head and spoke unintelligible Draconic, then demanded the slayer’s description. The witnesses spoke their account: dark armor, a black cloak, a pointed helm, and a glaive sparking green. Terror took the borrowed face. They were all dead, he said — thousands of years dead. The Dragonslayers of Raim.

A scream in the distance broke the sorrow. Vaerek, possessed, snatched Valeburn and ran. Saoirse followed her warden’s body; the rest of the group came after, through cream-white dunes, until they found the second body. Pethengorom still breathed. Blood poured from his mouth onto the Celestial Sands and one claw was gone. Montmorian stood over him with a wing lowered.

Saoirse emptied her staff of its restorative power. Perstrin copied what he could. Telorin took the leaf into his shaking hands; the raw infinite power nearly unmade him before Filibeth answered and asked whether she should bring them back.

They gathered and Montmorian became a man again, tears of rage in his eyes. Warmth took them to the Athag’ir Godtree. Filibeth’s pink-white flood closed sinew and crystal while Vaerek’s scaly triage invigorated the wounded dragon. Pethengorom resumed human shape, right hand missing, blood at the mouth, eyes open.

The Weaver spoke. A knight, he said. Magically silent. Pain in the back, then again. He had swatted, felt a blast of power, seen blood fall from the sky, and hardened his scales to gemstone. The dragonslayer had left him to bleed out. Cadarinost was a corpse. The word hit him like a second wound.

Perstrin and Telorin anchored a portal to the sanctuary and they carried the Weaver through. Heldranc waited at the foot of the throne — the ancient face showed fear. Fifteen stewards laid Pethengorom on cushions. Aszurithice cried that he lived. Nyralshirad stood silent. Heldranc asked two questions: whether the cloak had been black, and whether the helm had a spike. The group confirmed these truths once more.

It was a nightmare returned, he said. They were describing the armor of one man in particular. The ebon raiment of the Lord High Dragonslayer of the Empire of Raim, the commander by whose order dragonkind was exterminated. His true name was lost. Among dragons he was known only as Eig, the draconic word for fear.

Galand then spoke of the pit in the caverns. In the dark he had seen Vorn on a green plain, channeling the night that sent forth the Revenants, and he had answered with the light within him — an infinitesimal vestige of Caurobor, the Light Soul, the same light that lived in the Alabaster Blade.

The slayer was not of Vorn. The host was. They were fallen of Fala, displaced, wearing Montmorian’s crest, corrupted by the whispers of the Dark and answering the Beckoning Bell of Enthar. The same whispers that had already reached for Wren.

Pethengorom would rest, then reach for Darcdressil and Thalancord. Night fell on Nathalor. Telorin went to the forge with a phantasmal servant. In the aviary that opened on winter sky, Vaerek taught the air Heldranc’s fury, producing a cone of sonic force that threw gravel into the dark night. Galand joined with Rallice’s sword. As allies, they dueled, as they once did upon their first meeting. Rock cracked and blood marked the stone — two men exhibiting their strength and ever-growing might, readying themselves for the certain wars to come.

Act III, Chapter XXIX

The fight ran both sides of the dark. Magical night poured from a pit that dropped without bottom. Revenants climbed it like a tide. Montmorian’s Alabaster Blade threw white light as he cut them down and kicked them back into the drop. Vaerek flew the ledge, Valeburn burning as it struck, and called warning as more souls rose from below. He dropped to a wounded Phantom and dragged her back from the edge.

Galand’s eyes went pale gold. Wings of pure light unfurled from his back — Telorin’s work made flesh — and the cavern flashed. He dove through the wall of dark, hammer first, then lightning-swapped to Rallice’s greatsword. He rose again trailing light like a fading star.

Telorin flanked and put his rapier through one revenant; electricity leapt to the next and both fell. Then he cast Sanguine Mist into the press. The undead drank the void and stood taller. He dropped the spell the moment he understood. Saoirse lashed vitality through the dark, then blessed all allies. Six bodies moved with sudden surety — Arleth’s peace in the step. She called them around the pit to the far fight, where Phantoms were already dying.

Galand flew into the void alone. What he saw there he kept. He came back out, hammer sparking, and the last of the near host broke. Darkness receded. Torchlight returned. The pit was empty of souls, as if the night had never been.

Montmorian stood among the fallen in Fala plate and stared until his eyes glazed. He billowed his cloak. Vaerek healed who he could. To the surviving Phantoms he said they had fought well, and to burn the bodies with respect — these had been people taken. Montmorian did not look back.

They walked a cavern of massacre. Void had been here, killed everyone, and left no trace. The tunnel climbed toward heat and morning. They stepped out onto the Celestial Sands. White alabaster dunes. Twin suns. No rain. No thunder.

A dragon cried across the sky. It was not Pethengorom. Sand-colored, desert-scaled, it came screeching. Montmorian’s mouth found a smirk — then Vaerek saw the truth. The dragon’s soul was dim, dimmer even than the host. Paired with it rode a soul as bright as a lord’s.

The flight failed. Wings folded. The beast tumbled and struck the dunes a thousand feet out. Sand slammed the party. When the dust settled, Cadarinost lay dying. An armored man stood on the corpse, cape billowing, glaive crackling green. The magic pulled from the wound. Vaerek watched the dragon’s soul climb into the slayer. Cadarinost’s head fell.

Montmorian whispered Brother. Then he screamed it. Vaerek felt the loss in the old bond with dragonkind; steam rose from his eyes instead of tears.

Montmorian ran. Light and fire burst as he took his true form and landed on the dunes, throat filling with flame. A green oval opened behind the slayer. He stepped back through it. The portal closed. He was gone.

Act III, Chapter XXVIII

Vaerek climbed the hewn stairs with four Phantoms and peered through the rubble at the cave mouth. Rain still poured. Fire still burned. Pethengorom’s flame would not be quelled — blue at the core, crystalline and sorcerous. A few dim souls lingered among the dead. The dragon himself was gone.

Vaerek told the Phantoms the truth: the mage who had saved them could take a dragon’s form. Their fear broke into awe, then hope. He called Pethengorom’s name into the storm, but was not answered.

Below, the party chose to leave the Phantoms in their sanctuary and press north. Taleera told them more of the host: empty eyes, black hoods, a vigor gone. For years they had come in small, skulking bands. Only in the past year had they marched in numbers, organized, under a storm that had raged ceaselessly.

The caverns ran deep, lined with torches. The sheer pathway dropped into blackness. They walked for a time until two dwellers stopped Montmorian and pointed at his chest. The sigil on his Fala armor was the sigil of their enemy, the Revenants.

Telorin spoke the thought no one wanted: perhaps the Revenant Host were Falans taken in the Fading, corrupted by something darker. Montmorian’s stare went distant. Saoirse turned and trudged back to the entrance to see with her own eyes.

Outside, the canyon was a sea of burning dead. Dragon-fire did not touch her. She turned bodies until she found what she feared: blackened plate that held the symbol of the Holy Three, cloaks dyed or charred over a blue that Rallice had worn. She cut a breastplate free and tore a scrap of the old color. In the thunder she paused and asked Arleth if that sea of death was what the pilgrimage had meant. Ulric’s warning returned to her: the path she walked would lead to ruin. She fled the moans of the dying back into the cave.

She put the proof in Montmorian’s hands. He stared a long time. Then he swore that whoever had done this would feel their wrath, and with a shout he hurled plate and cloak into the dark below.

They walked on along a ledge above a drop that had no bottom. Vaerek heard movement far down — too large for vermin — and then a pulse that was not natural. Galand heard it next. Darkness poured through the cavern like a tide, swallowing torchlight. Magic filled the air, high and dark, with no single source. Souls rose from the pit and reached the edge.

Montmorian drew the Sword of Torados, an ancient alabaster blade of a Val'norain that threw its own light. Vaerek’s cloak split into wings. Telorin laid haste on Galand. The host stepped out of the dark as if walking on open air.

Montmorian did not hold back. The glowing sword cut and kicked the first revenant back into the dark. Galand’s hammer answered with thunder. Vaerek struck from above. Saoirse, for the first time, went on the offensive: an icicle grown with Aszurithice’s precision, vitality sparking as it lodged in a throat. They were undead.

The fight spilled along the ledge. Revenants swung with empty, inhuman strength; void-magic slid off Galand, ineffective. Phantoms fought at their side. One of their guides fell. Saoirse put a glowing hand on the survivor’s shoulder and told him to strike for his friend.

Telorin, back to the dark, heard whispers from within. He answered with a cloud of rust. Vaerek healed, called Heldranc’s name, and rose again. An orb of light bloomed deeper in the cavern. What more waited in the dark was unknown.

Act III, Chapter XXVII

In Akath, the travelers took shelter in ancient ruins while the unnatural storm raged. Lightning blasted the wet dunes without pause. Pethengorom raised his protective prism and then left them, climbing a ruined tower. In a flash of lightning and a draconic cry he took true form. His wings and back were pure living crystal, refracting the storm and casting reflections of the ceaseless lightning. He flew north to survey the desert and search the current.

While they waited, Montmorian spoke of Fala. He had arrived after the fall of the Eren Dominion, rose through the ranks - known as the 'Gifted Knight' whose youth never faded - and been crowned when King Gadros was lost. He embraced the ideals of the Holy Three, and over the next forty years, built the greatest empire the world had known. Then, it was taken from him in a single day. All could see on his face the undeniable trauma that lingered. The power that destroyed it, he said, was beyond all power that he knew.

Pethengorom returned at dusk. The desert was occupied with a marching army - black-armored figures moving in disciplined squads, souls dim and wrong. When he made himself known, the lightning struck harder as if the storm itself answered. The host was heading north. They would wait in the ruins until Pethengorom gave them further instruction.

A week passed under constant thunder. Vaerek and Saoirse explored the ruins and found faded paint that once might have belonged to Fena. On the ninth day the host turned into a canyon that marked the border of the Celestial Sands. Pethengorom teleported the party to the ridge above them.

Below, the army marched under a pocket of clear air while the storm poured everywhere else. A cave mouth waited in their path. It was deemed likely that the host sought Cadarinost. If the host entered that cavern, draconic power would be useless against them. Montmorian and the party would go in first. Pethengorom would remain outside and deal with the army.

They ran. Galand toppled stone into the canyon. Saoirse raised vines and a wall of wind across the entrance. Pethengorom’s shriek split the sky; his magical fire spewed forth and rolled down the canyon and the host burned. The survivors who tried to reach the cave found only barriers.

Inside, the cavern grew colder and quieter. Stairs descended into worked stone. Campfires burned ahead. Vaerek’s Draconic Sight showed two living souls beside each fire - souls that ordinary eyes could not see. Saoirse approached with open hands and the calm of Arleth. One figure stepped out of invisibility, then others. Through Perstrin’s translation they named themselves the Phantoms, servants of Cadarinost.

The Revenant Host had plagued them for generations, but only in the last year had its numbers swollen from dozens to hundreds. The host stole their children with black magic; the faces returned years later empty of recognition. Cadarinost had granted them the gift of invisibility and charged them with defending the southern blooms of the Celestial Sands. He himself had gone north to those blooms a month ago and never returned.

The host that had marched on the canyon was now dead or dying. A Phantom woman named Taleera offered a cautious welcome. Outside, the storm still raged. Ahead, the blooms waited, and with them, Cadarinost.

Act III, Chapter XXVI

Everyone gathered in the common chamber in the evening. Vaerek entered with Saoirse, a blindfold across his eyes, yet he moved with a new certainty. Hearing and smell had been drawn tight as a dragon’s; he knew the ripple of the pool, the hush of whispered talk, the approaching steps in the hall.

He pulled the cloth down. Where his eyes should have been burned two fiery orbs.

Heldranc had given him draconic sight - the way the Kindred see in their true forms. The world came to him in shades of flame: outlines, frames, the twinkling cores of souls. Most were ordinary, shifting like stars. Galand’s was different - a condensed, ordered radiance of white-gold, contained rather than crackling. Filibeth’s light seemed to follow the shape of her whole being. Vaerek lingered on them a moment, then covered his eyes again. He would wear the blindfold now, and among strangers. The orbs were too much to show the world.

Filibeth was glad that he was in fighting shape once more.

Morning found Telorin already in the forge, shirtless, goggles on. Saoirse arrived with her spellbook. Together they finished a wand: Telorin completed the last of the rune, and she channeled a Circle of Protection spell into the wood in Wildword. When she tested it upon him, a wave of Arleth’s calm settled over him - a peace so deep it felt like sedation. He understood, for a moment, why she had given her life to that goddess.

Galand’s tattoo would wait for private work: great wing-shapes across his back, an ambitious fusion of ink and a rechargeable arcane effect.

At midday the Lords convened in the throne room. Tension still hung between Montmorian and Aszurithice. She spoke of the Underrealm.

Deep beneath the Winterlands the caverns opened onto a frozen underground sea. Souls walked the ice and rose like vapor - one mind, one purpose, one hivemind. In service to Celurna, she had frozen them and wrung the same answers from every one. They served Ito Gangara: the Hungerer, Queen of the Core, Queen of the Void. She dwelt beneath the ice deeper still. So long as she remained there, the hive would rise and kill and grow.

South of that sea lay the Silver Forest, a place of ensorcelled silver-leaved trees and dark shadows. Beyond it, a white desert and a kingdom she had not entered. Saoirse’s eyes widened. Wren had dreamed of silver trees. Filibeth remembered a Spectre King her father had once meant to invade.

Heldranc set the course. The realm below belonged to such demons. They must first strengthen the realm above. Cadarinost was next: a proud, reclusive brother who had made himself a hidden archive in the sands, and who had spoken of something called the Revenant Host.

Nyralshirad would accompany them. Then, after debate, Pethengorom as well i his knowledge of souls and his affinity for sand better suited than Nyralshirad’s impatience to fight.

Saoirse asked the Lords how they wished to be seen. She spoke of Vaerek’s fire that could cleanse as well as scour, of Yollonde’s queen who would not ally with her ancestors’ oppressors, of Darkdressil already named Terror of the Depths. She claimed that if the dragons returned only as flame, the world would be as the K'bas, and that they needed to prove they were not Mano.

To this Montmorian and Nyralshirad took offense. The Dragonlord of the Woodland cared little for mortal perception. He explained that demons and their thralls were the enemy, those who should be killed by any means. Pethengorom offered to travel in his place. Montmorian, kingly and raw beneath the surface, granted that they would discern the souls before they struck - and that the K'bas would never happen again. In a sharp and assertive tone, he spoke that he would not be spoken to as a servant, reminding Saoirse and the rest that, though they were appreciated, they were not equals. This was a marked change in demeanor from what the servants had grown accustomed to. Time was running out and a life had been lost.

They stepped through Pethengorom’s portal into Uma Sura. It was raining.

Thunder rolled without pause. Lightning walked the wet dunes, leaving blackened craters and glass in the sand. Pethengorom had never read of rain in these lands. He raised a crystal bubble from his staff and the storm sheeted off it while they trudged through the muck toward old white-sandstone ruins.

This was Akath, the southern reaches. North lay the true white sands, and somewhere among them, they hoped, Cadarinost.

They took shelter under weathered stone as lightning and thunder held the sky, and the next day’s journey waited.

Act III, Chapter XXV

The blizzard had quieted. In the sudden stillness Galand rolled the body over.

Rallice was dead.

Saoirse knelt beside him, one hand cradling his head, the other searching in vain for a pulse that would never return. Blackened veins traced outward from his eyes, as though he had gazed upon something so absolute that it had simply unmade him. Vaerek, still blinded, stumbled toward the sound of their voices and sank to one knee in the snow.

Aszurithice approached through the calming weather, her form once more human, pale as frost, armor trailing thin veils of cold. She looked down at the fallen knight.

“Not necromancy,” she said, voice low and ethereal. “He gazed upon the Void.”

She asked permission to guard the soul. Vaerek made space without hesitation. Aszurithice knelt, laid her hand upon Rallice’s brow, and spoke in draconic. Frost came from her fingertips, sealing the body against further decay. When she rose, the finality of it settled over them all.

Telorin’s hallucination finally fractured under the weight of contradiction. One moment he stood alone in a private void; the next the world snapped back into place and he saw them gathered around the body. He stumbled forward through the snow and sank to his knees beside the others.

They could not remain on the open ridge. Saoirse called upon Filibeth through the leaf. The portal opened. Warmth and the scent of stone replaced the howling cold of Frostfell Reach as the party stepped back into the sanctuary of Nathalor, Galand still carrying Rallice in his arms.

Filibeth met them in the throne room. The Dragonlords gathered. Montmorian arrived last, hope on his face turning to devastation the moment he saw the body.

Rage followed close behind sorrow.

He turned on Aszurithice. Why had she not answered the summons? Why had a knight of Fala died while she remained silent? Aszurithice met the accusation without flinching. She had been deep in the Underrealm, hunting an entrance to the Core, concealing herself from the scrying of Ito Gangara - the Hungerer whose spirits stole souls and bound them into a hive of the dead. The magic they wielded was of the Void itself: older than the Infinite, anathema to every living soul, capable of warping mind and destroying it outright. Only two nights earlier had she returned to the surface.

Nyralshirad delivered the second blow. Two of her brothers - Vabaldinur and Salhinurash - were dead.

Aszurithice’s glacial composure cracked for the first time.

Montmorian’s fury did not cool. He demanded purpose for the death. He demanded that no more of their servants fall while the Lords remained in hiding. Then he left the chamber, carrying Rallice’s body.

Galand had already slipped away. Somewhere deeper in the halls a great crash echoed - stone and rubble, the sound of his grief breaking stone.

Saoirse examined Vaerek’s eyes in the quiet that followed. No magic lingered to be dispelled. The blindness was physical yet unnatural: the same dark, clouded streaks that had marked Rallice’s death, only lesser. She could not promise a cure.

The party gathered in Telorin’s workshop, the forge still carrying the memory of Wren’s banter and Rallice’s bags of ingots left exactly where he had set them. Grief hung thick in the air. They turned to research instead, pouring over tomes that spoke of the Infinite and the darker framework beyond it - the Cosm, the Elder Magic of the Void. Pethengorom confirmed what they had already felt: this power did not answer to souls born of the Infinite. It corrupted. It controlled. It could not be mastered by ordinary means.

Telorin began work on a wide-brimmed hat for Perstrin, runes meant to sharpen recall and understanding of the arcane. The project was half practical, half desperate distraction.

Three days passed.

In the deep chamber of the Fallen, beneath torchlight and the glowing eyes of the Dragonlords, they laid Rallice to rest.

Montmorian spoke first. He had not known the knight long, but he had known his father, and Rallice had been a surviving sword of Fala - proof that the kingdom still lived. That sword was now sheathed. The duty would pass to those who remained.

Telorin laid a handmade crest of Fala beside the body. Saoirse spoke of integrity and the quiet strength that had marked the man. Others each said their remembrances. Filibeth placed a single leaf upon his chest. The stone lid slid into place.

Vaerek stayed longest, blind hands resting on the cool stone, unable even to look upon his friend one final time in his blindness.

Research continued. Pethengorom believed a warding against the Void might be possible for mortals. Something that shielded not body or mind, but their souls. He would speak with Heldranc. The party leaned toward the desert next, to search for Cadarinost. They would save Nybei for last.

A steward came for Vaerek. Heldranc requested his presence alone, high above, in a vast open chamber carved into the mountainside where the winter wind still howled across the threshold.

Saoirse released his hand to the steward. Vaerek went upward into the cold, leaving the others behind.

Act III, Chapter XXIV

Morning in the sanctuary was brief and the Kindred awoke readied for their next quest. The Six Kings had seen them in Syr Sable; whatever watched from the shadows might already be moving. They gathered once more to the chamber of the lords, stocked, geared, and clad in cold-weather armor prepared by the Lords themselves.

Pethengorom stood forward as the others drew aside. He had touched the Current that morning and found their sister. Aszurithice lived. Her soul still burned with the same fierce light. He could not swear to her safety, only that she still fought with her own will. The cause of her disappearance remained unknown.

The Lords would not travel with them. Pethengorom and Nyralshirad had business of their own in Aelyn, the land which Vabaldinur, the Warden once guarded. The travelers' path lay elsewhere.

Pethengorom opened the portal. Green magical light flared and a blast of absolute cold tore into the chamber. The Lords bowed their heads in farewell. Vaerek met Heldranc’s eyes one last time, offered Filibeth a quiet word, and stepped through.

They emerged into Frostfell Reach.

Wind howled across a world of white. Snow came to the knees with every step. The suns were a dim rumor behind an unending icy storm. Saoirse left Aoifa behind in the sanctuary; the hawk would not have survived. Lanterns replaced torches. Visibility shrank to almost nothing. For nearly two hours they trudged north-northwest through the blizzard, following the faint pull of Aszurithice’s presence.

Then the whispers began - thin, unintelligible voices carried on the wind. Moments later the snow itself rose into tall spectral forms wielding ghostly blades, their eyes piercing blue-light orbs.

Combat was immediate and brutal.

The ghosts struck in wide, sweeping emanations. Steel passed through them as though they were mist. Only magic and spirit-touched force seemed to bite. Vaerek’s dragonfire carved brief tunnels of light through the storm. Telorin’s spellstrikes and Saoirse’s verdant rays found purchase. Galand’s greathammer, when his eyes began to glow with that strange divine spark, connected as though the spirits were flesh. He and Rallice struggled in their heavy armor already rimed with frost.

A spectral sorcerer lingered somewhere in the white, casting suggestions that forced Telorin to extinguish his own lights and later isolated him in a private hallucination of solitude. Wave after wave pressed in. Perstrin cast a spell of flight, to escape the melee. Rallice fell to the ground. Saoirse poured healing from her staff and made hasty prayers to Arleth. The party was driven to the edge of collapse.

Then the wind itself changed.

A vast, freezing gust swept the ridge. The blizzard parted. From the newly cleared air came a piercing, bone-deep shriek that echoed across the mountains. The remaining ghosts turned from the party as if in retreat. A colossal shape of pure frost and scale emerged upon the cliff edge. Absolute cold poured from its open maw, a luminous blue beam which rimed the entire mountainside in crystalline ice. But... the blessing of the Kindred held, as it did in Athag'ir. The Kindred felt the cold but were not harmed.

The dragon’s form flew upward, then descended further down the pass. In a great vortex of swirling snow, the form collapsed and disappeared. Where it had stood then stood a woman in armor of living ice, black hair streaming in the wind, and eyes the piercing blue of glacial depths.

She looked upon Vaerek, then the rest, then spoke.

“Pleasure to meet thee, Vaerek Rathkin. I have heard of thine endeavors.”

Her voice carried easily across the storm. Through the lingering magical distortion that still clouded Telorin’s senses, the words also found him, as though she addressed him instead of the Drakewarden.

“My kin have called upon me, but I have not heeded their call. For I fight against Ito Gangara. My presence here is needed.”

Behind her, the last of the spectral forms dissolved into nothing. The party stood in the sudden stillness, breath steaming, weapons still raised. Rallice lay motionless in the snow where he had fallen for the final time. Galand rolled him onto his back, and gazed upon his eyes, destroyed by some manner of killing magic. The life had already fled from him despite every desperate heal Saoirse could pour into his body. She shouted his name, they held him to awaken, but, the Falan knight was gone.

Act III, Chapter XXIII

The Kindred entered the grand marble throne room of Queen Primwyn, flanked by royal guards. The chamber was a display of wealth and power: high ceilings, masterwork sconces, and heavy red tapestries framed the upholstered stone throne. The queen sat in regal attire, her foreign-born attendant standing close at hand. Foggy daylight poured through tall windows that overlooked the city and bay.

The queen greeted them formally, already briefed on their names, origins, and claims. She cut straight to the matter: they had spoken of a cure for the Red Scourge. Telorin clarified that they offered no ready-made remedy for the afflicted, but rather a path to stop the source itself - believing that ending the origin might free those already cursed. Saoirse and the others expanded: they sought Darcdressil, the Palemoon King, whom they believed still lived within the Ochre Depths, near the root of the curse. They named the Six Kings as the likely architects of the Scourge, spreading it through dreams. Vaerek added that his own draconic vigor agitated the afflicted, offering another thread of connection.

When the queen pressed on how such ancient figures could still endure, the party spoke carefully of long-lived lords and powerful blood. Saoirse eventually revealed the Godtree leaf, linking it to Sar Sareen’s scriptures and the tree that once served as a conduit of divine power. She argued that with the leaf, draconic strength, and an army’s support, a path into the Depths might finally be forced open - allowing Yollonde to liberate its people as Sar Sareen once had.

Queen Primwyn listened with growing gravity. She confirmed much of what they suspected: the Six Kings still lingered in the wasteland, deformed and undying; Darcdressil, known to her realm as the dracoonic 'Terror of the Depths', fought them endlessly within the dunes; and the Scourge spread through cursed dreams. Yet she refused any call to raise banners. Marching an army a month’s journey into the Ochre Depths would leave her kingdom undefended and cost countless lives. She preferred to focus on developing elixirs that induced dreamless sleep, ensuring her people relief.

The conversation turned sharper when the party revealed their communion with living dragons -King Montmorian of Fala among them - and their mission to reunite the scattered Dragon Lords. Rallice knelt and testified that he had seen his own king alive and allied with the Lords. Vaerek displayed his draconic blood through his growing primal form. The grand revelation stirred the Queen as she leaned forward with great worry and shock. Saoirse spoke with passion of hope, renewal, and second chances, drawing parallels between Arleth’s defiance of a predatory world and the Kindred’s belief that the dragons could yet choose a better path. The queen remained unmoved. History, she said, showed only that dragons brought scorching war and scarred lands. She would not ally her people with such power, no matter the sincerity of the travelers before her. She wished them victory against the Scourge, but dismissed them as fools for serving dragons.

Outside the castle, the party processed the outcome. They had confirmed Darcdressil’s identity and location, learned the true scale of the threat, and gained allies in Greymoor, yet they lacked the force to press further. Telorin and others argued that continuing alone would be suicide; better to return to the Enclave, seek the remaining Dragonlords, and build strength before any assault on the Depths. Perstrin commented on the extraordinarily peculiar absence of magic within the throne room. Brigh and Jiatra agreed the queen’s caution was sound from a ruler’s perspective. Warm farewells were exchanged. The hunters of Greymoor departed to seek apothecaries and dream-potions, while the Kindred sought a quiet alley to contact Filibeth.

The leaf glowed. Filibeth answered and drew them home through the familiar sensation of warmth and weightlessness. They reappeared in the sanctuary of basalt and stone, greeted by Filibeth and the presence of Heldranc. Montmorian soon joined them. The party delivered their report: the queen’s refusal, the confirmation that Darcdressil remained trapped and possibly corrupted within the Ochre Depths, the Six Kings’ active use of a Godtree to spread the Scourge through dreams, and the necessity of greater force. The Dragonlords were not pleased with the reveal of their existence, though some viewed it as inevitable.

Heldranc and Montmorian absorbed the news grimly. The Six Kings still lived; Darcdressil would never be safe alone. War would eventually be required, but first the remaining kin must be found. The next destination was set: The Winterlands, where Aszurithice was last known to aid the Champions of Celurna against the shamans of Gangar. Preparations for the frigid journey would begin in short time. Wren’s departure was noted with quiet concern. Private conversations followed - while the rest of the Kindred rested, the weight of the failed audience and the long road ahead settled over them.

Act III, Chapter XXII

The party departed Greymoor with Sir Brigh and Lady Jiatra as guides and holy representatives. The road to Yollonde City stretched nearly a moon's cycle beneath ceaseless drizzle. Red Scourge encounters thinned nearer populated lands, yet each clash carried grim weight. Vaerek tested his draconic vigor against the afflicted, noting their agitation to his healing flames. Galand's divine spark flared in battle, echoing the crimson dreams that lingered. Brigh shared fireside lore of Sar Sareen, while Jiatra's prayers warded the night's chill. Wren's absence weighed heavily; distant lute songs in inns only sharpening the memory of his sudden departure.

At the bay, they boarded small sailboats for the final crossing. The island capital emerged from the mist—stone spires, lantern-lit harbors bustling with merchants and royal guards. The rain never ceased, but the sight of ordered civilization brought weary relief. Customs passed smoothly, aided by the mayor's sealed letter and the hunters' reputation. They made straight for the Hall of Diplomats, presenting their case for urgent audience regarding the Red Scourge. They displayed their sincerity through piety and foreign connection to the Mighty and Immortal Fala.

Before their formal audience, the group sought respite in a tavern. In a secluded booth, conversation turned candid and unguarded. The travelers explained their journey and origins to the priestess and paladin. Saoirse revealed the enchanted Godtree leaf, presenting it to Jiatra as a sign of good faith, a real symbol matching the legends of Sareen. Within minutes, a presence awakened, and whispers filled the air - chanting voices invoking the Six Kings. "Amirian. Tath. Imentos. Valinvoren. Hetraxis. Kaliant." The leaf glowed with immense power, resisting movement or dispel. Raw infinity surged, threatening to teleport them as they had been before. Galand slumped unconscious. Vaerek, intervening with his vigor, suffered a blackout - his eyes glowing red before fading, leaving him dazed with a splitting headache.

Chaos gripped the booth. Perstrin channeled his mimicry, absorbing and stabilizing the wild magic as Telorin and Saoirse layered dispels and blessings. Brigh and Jiatra prayed fervently. Telorin focused on the voices and questioned them. The voices spoke of eternal power and the Palemoon, hinting at Darcdressil. Through desperate communion, Saoirse reached Filibeth through the leaf once more. The princess's guidance anchored them, her voice cutting through the storm and providing stability. The surge subsided without displacing them, for the first time. Now it was seen, the leaf could channel both peril and salvation.

Shaken, the Kindred reflected. Galand awoke. Perstrin realized his tattoos had absorbed the leaf's power, granting him the ability to mimic and wield such immense magic in the future. The encounter forged deeper unity and underscored the Godtree's perilous centrality. Saoirse offered protective blessings as they retired and the tavern's warmth provided sanctuary against the constant rain.

The next morning, they returned to the Hall of Diplomats. With approved documentation in hand, they were escorted through the upper districts to the royal castle. The queen's handmaiden received them, confirming their credentials as Falan dignitaries and Scourgeslayers. They entered the grand receiving hall, where Queen Primwyn awaited on her throne.

Act III, Chapter XXI

After the harrowing night in the ruined chapel of Astar and the difficult burial of the fallen hunters, the party found welcome respite in the Guildhall of the Scourgeslayers in the village of Greymoor. For the first time in many days, they slept in warm, clean beds beneath a roof adorned with countless sigils and talismans of Sar Sareen. No nightmares plagued them that night—perhaps a subtle blessing of the goddess of restoration watched over the hall.

Dawn brought gentle rain. Saoirse rose first and approached Ment, the guarded Mageslayer, who sat writing in his journal at a table. She offered a quiet “Good morning” and inquired about his thoughts. He spoke solemnly of recording the loss of their comrades, acknowledging the dangers they had long accepted. The rest of the party stirred soon after. Saoirse gently woke the others, including Galand and Telorin from the upper loft, and shared the discovery pinned to the door: a farewell letter from Wren.

She read the bard’s words aloud to the gathered group. Wren confessed that the whispers from his dreams had only grown louder since arriving in this new world, accompanied by darkness from his past. He believed himself a danger to his companions and had set out alone to master whatever afflicted him, hoping one day their paths might cross again. The party reacted with shock and sorrow. Vaerek in particular noted how inconsolable Wren had seemed after the shared dreams. Though some suggested tracking him down, others respected his need for solitude, likening it to a druid’s quiet walk. Prayers were offered that the Red Scourge would not claim him first and that he might find his way back.

With Wren’s departure weighing on them, conversation turned to their broader quest. Saoirse and others pressed Sir Brigh and Lady Jiatra for more on local lore. Brigh recounted the scriptures of Sar Sareen: how the goddess, leading the remnants of the fallen Dalamand monarchy, was blessed by a magnificent tree during the war against the Six Kings. Through this tree she drew strength, banished the tyrannical kings to the southern Ochre Depths, and ascended to divinity. In exchange, the tree itself was consumed by the kings’ corrupting magic, leaving the wasteland a place of doom; horrors, beasts, and forbidden ground no mortal should tread. The motif of the tree, blended with water and restoration, adorned the guildhall.

The revelation that the Godtree of legend lay within the cursed Ochre Depths sent ripples through the party. Vaerek and Telorin pressed on the dangers, while Galand and Rallice gauged the threat. Brigh and the hunters warned sternly that entering the Depths without an army was suicide. The Red Scourge itself was suspected to originate from that blighted place, possibly still influenced by the banished kings. Plans shifted: the group resolved to travel to Yollonde’s capital to seek an audience with Queen Primwyn, present their case, and potentially secure aid - or even bring their draconic benefactor into the fray.

Before departing, they met with Mayor Callenrose in his cozy hall. The mayor, a childhood friend of Brigh, listened gravely. Though skeptical of their wild claims, he honored Brigh’s trust and provided a formal letter of introduction bearing Greymoor’s seal. Pouches of Red Scourge eyes - grisly trophies - were exchanged as tokens of the hunters’ ongoing vigilance. Saoirse and Vaerek spent hours in the guildhall’s tomes, poring over scriptures. Saoirse found detailed accounts of the tree’s role in Sar Sareen’s ascension and the banishment; Vaerek sought (with limited success) specifics on the Ochre Depths’ monsters and curses - mostly dire warnings to stay away.

In a quiet moment, Saoirse used Filibeth’s enchanted leaf to reach the Glaurfeldt princess. The connection held despite the distance. She updated Filibeth on their progress, the suspected location of a Godtree in the Ochre Depths, and the dire warnings about the region. Filibeth promised to relay the information to Heldranc and the other Dragonlords, offering potential assistance. Saoirse also shared what they had learned of the Red Scourge’s possible ties to the Six Kings.

The letter was delivered by the mayor's boy of ten years, an aspiring Scourgehunter. He marveled at Galand’s mighty greathammer, which only the Glaurfeldt warrior could lift, and the newcomers' displays of magic. Saoirse and others offered gentle guidance, emphasizing the value of protecting one’s people in many ways. Brigh and Jiatra agreed to accompany the party toward the capital as guides, representatives of their faith, and protectors, while Krim and Ment remained to safeguard Greymoor.

As the rain continued, the party prepared to depart at dawn for the multi-week journey to the capital. Skirmishes with the Red Scourge along the way were expected; Vaerek intended to test his draconic vigor further against the afflicted. With a letter from the mayor, the blessing of Sar Sareen’s faithful, and a tenuous link to their northern patrons, they set forth once more, hopeful that this path would lead not only to Darcdressil but to a way to stem the Red Scourge itself.

Act III, Chapter XX

In the somber hours following the violent confrontation, the party and the surviving Scourgeslayers of Greymoor gathered in the chapel dedicated to Astar, the dead god of twilight. The golden radiance of Saoirse’s rituals slowly receded as she knelt near the group, checking on the survivors. The weight of the night pressed heavily; the hunters had lost three longtime companions, Greyguard Talke, Swordsman Tenn, and Manhunter Leonis, to the Red Scourge without warning.

Saoirse let out a solemn sigh and questioned the surviving hunters on why their fallen comrades had succumbed. The group stood in stunned grief. Vaerek, still smoldering from the dragonfire he had unleashed on the afflicted Tenn, looked down at the smoking corpse and stared grimly. The party debated how much to reveal about the nature of their quest. After nods of trust from Galand, Saoirse, and the others, acknowledging that the hunters had fought alongside them and lost their own, they decided the survivors deserved the truth.

Vaerek spoke carefully, starting softly: the party served a powerful draconic lord from the frozen north, one of the last Dragonlords. They sought his lost son, another wielder of draconic power, while investigating and combating the Red Scourge. He emphasized their protective role and that they meant no harm, looking the hunters earnestly in the eyes. He recounted attempting to purge the Scourge from Leonis using his vigor-healing abilities, only for the afflicted man to speak, something previously unheard of. The flames Vaerek had unleashed carried draconic essence, perhaps anathema to the Scourge.

Inside the chapel, Saoirse approached Jiatra, offering help and joining her in quiet prayer and condolences for the fallen. She assured the silent priestess that both Arleth and Sar Sareen would hear the names of their lost friends. Saoirse also examined the three statues in the altar chamber: Nadra, Goddess of Night and Moon, Astar, God of Twilight and Transformation, and Fena, Goddess of Day and Sun. She noted the symbolic gathering of celestial forces and reflected on her fallen friend Corvyr, a follower of Fena. The twilight outside and the events of the evening suggested lingering divinity.

As night deepened, preparations for rest began. Galand and Sir Brigh ventured out into the pouring rain to dig graves. The young Glaurfeldt warrior worked steadily despite the downpour. Brigh bonded with him, expressing deep gratitude for his aid in the fight and sensing a divine spark within Galand. He urged the young man to keep his heart open to whatever god called to him. Galand mentioned his past connection to Mano. Brigh shared his own history of faith and loss.

Inside, Vaerek gathered tattered cloth to serve as respectful burial shrouds. The bodies were laid out with dignity, hands clasped, eyes closed, despite the grim necessity of their earlier destruction. Saoirse left a vial of holy water wrapped in vinery on the altar as an offering - for her old friend Coryvr as much as it was also for the Gods.

Eventually, the group settled down to sleep in a drier section of the chapel, away from the bloodstained floor. In their sleep, dark dreams plagued them.

Saoirse found herself before a strange pink tree unlike those she knew. Three indistinct figures stood before it; roots spread in all directions. She felt deep worry, fear, and helplessness, as if something fundamental was wrong.

Vaerek dreamed as if he were Heldranc the Dragonlord. Fire raged everywhere and hundreds of bodies were strewn about, beneath the crimson moon. The presence of his patron overwhelmed him.

Wren walked through shadows in a dark forest. Whispers surrounded him, escalating into moaning and wailing. A solitary Godtree with silver leaves stood amid the gloom. The sound of a large bell ringing awakened him.

Telorin and Perstrin shared an identical vision: a green flame flickering in a great brazier. Beyond it, seven indistinct figures loomed. In the fire, a reflection of red eyes faded to dust.

Rallice relived the fall of his home city of Fala. Screams echoed as people disintegrated into dust around him.

Galand saw rays of light forming and then being enveloped by a crimson moon. He awoke in a stupor; Perstrin, already awake, informed him that his eyes had glowed red for a moment before fading.

The party awoke unrested. Saoirse gently roused Vaerek, noting his white-knuckled grip on his sword and warmer-than-usual breath. They shared their dreams quietly at first. Vaerek described becoming Heldranc amid fire and a crimson moon. Saoirse spoke of the pink tree and roots. Wren appeared deeply distraught, stroking his beard and staring off. Vaerek openly asked if everyone shared the experience.

As they discussed the shared visions - green braziers, silver Godtrees, crimson moons, and red eyes - Wren recounted his nightmare of whispers turning to wails, the silver Godtree, and the bell that woke him - twice, he claimed, though the ruined chapel had no functioning bell. Saoirse suggested writing the dreams down while they were fresh in their memories. Rallice provided pages from an old field notebook. The group recorded the details carefully.

Galand confided in Sir Brigh about his glowing red eyes. The paladin listened cautiously but supportively, offering to help monitor and understand the divine, and potentially ominous, spark within him. Conversations turned to possible connections between the dreams, the Red Scourge, the fading of gods, and larger forces at play. Saoirse asked Jiatra and Brigh about dispelling magic on the Scourged; attempts had limited effect, suggesting a power on par with the Fading itself. Telorin theorized the source might rival Mano’s strength. Brigh explained that the hunters only dreamt in peace since they were blessed by Sareen.

With the dead properly shrouded and graves prepared, the group offered final respects. They then set out on the road toward Greymoor, a four-day journey on foot. The hunters guided them through the rainy landscape of Yollonde.

Upon arrival, Greymoor appeared as a dilapidated but cozy village. The returning Scourgeslayers were greeted warmly by commonfolk in the streets, praised as local heroes. The party reached the Hunters’ guildhall, adorned with holy symbols of Sar Sareen. Gratitude was expressed for the shelter and aid provided in the chapel.

Act III, Chapter XIX

The party pressed onward along the muddy roads of Syr Sable, accompanied by the wary Hunters of Greymoor. Rain fell in fitful bursts, soaking the endless fields and hills that stretched toward distant mountains. The hunters rode in loose formation, some ahead and others circling back, their vigilance never wavering as they watched for any sign of the dreaded Red Scourge. They were always alert, always circling, ensuring the safety of the group in this dangerous land.

After three quiet nights of travel - with Wren offering no songs for the group and the campfires burning low and subdued - the fourth day brought them to evening. As the party settled around the fire, Vaerek suggested that he and Wren take the first watch. Wren agreed with a wry smile, remarking that he had always been something of a night owl.

As the others drifted off to sleep, Vaerek’s tone grew serious. He asked Wren if he was alright, recalling the strange trance that had overtaken him a few nights earlier while playing a song. Wren had seemed lost, his eyes unfocused, as if he had slipped away into some deep, fearful absence. Wren tried to brush it off as nothing more than a passionate performance, the kind of concentration that came naturally to him when playing. But Vaerek was not convinced. He pressed gently, noting that Wren rarely spoke of his past - only passing mentions, never a full story. It felt like quiet omission rather than deliberate avoidance. Vaerek made it clear he was not trying to force confessions or twist arms; he simply wanted to know his companion was safe. The conversation ended with mutual assurances of friendship and support, though an undercurrent of mystery lingered in the firelight.

The morning came, and the party returned to the road. They traveled all day until evening, when the rains returned once more. As twilight settled over the land, the first structure they had seen in four days appeared in the distance - the ruins of an old chapel nestled near trees off the road. The hunters announced they would rest there for the night. It was an ancient chapel of Astar, the dead god of twilight. The group took shelter inside, finding dry corners amid damaged pews and leaking roofs. As they settled in, conversation turned to the nature of gods and mortality. Brigh, the burly paladin of Sar Sareen, explained that gods rely on their worshippers. In his words, without hearts bent in reverence, a god loses power and has no pulse in the mortal world. Saoirse added her own perspective, describing divinity as a relationship between souls rather than pure immortal power. Vaerek and others debated the implications, wondering how gods could truly die and whether the same rules applied to demons or other entities.

As the night deepened, Saoirse and Ment conversed near the doorway, both taking first watch. Saoirse and Ment spoke quietly near the doorway, their voices low against the patter of rain. Ment asked about her connection to Aoife, her hawk companion. Saoirse explained that while she could not fully control the bird’s will, she could share sight with her through an Arlethian ritual. Ment observed that she did not carry the look of a northerner. Saoirse replied that her parents were not from the north and that her upbringing as a druid had been different. She mentioned her mother was a gifted shapeshifter who could take the form of ravens. Ment inquired what such a transformation felt like and looked like. Saoirse described willing shapeshifts as gentle and seamless, while forced ones were gruesome and painful.

The philosophical discussion was shattered by sudden horror. Three of the hunters - Talke, Tenn, and Leonis - rose with glowing red eyes, consumed by the Red Scourge. They attacked without hesitation, their movements unnaturally strong and devoid of pain. Chaos erupted in the tight confines of the ruined chapel. Jiatra shrieked as one loomed over her. Brigh dragged her to safety with grim determination. Krim and Ment sprang into action with deadly precision. The party leapt to fight alongside them.

Galand’s eyes began to glow with an eerie divine light as battle instincts took hold. He moved with fluid, unnatural grace, his greathammer striking with devastating force. Saoirse sustained and expanded her blessing, bathing the group in golden radiance, and attempted to dispel the curse afflicting the hunters. Vaerek dodged attacks from the enthralled and used his own blood in an experimental healing attempt. Telorin maneuvered skillfully, striking from the flanks with his rapier. Wren and Perstrin cast offensive sorcery as the skirmish raged.

The corrupted hunters fought with terrifying resilience, but they were eventually brought down through coordinated effort and holy power. Jiatra called a pillar of Sareen's holy light upon Talke. Vaerek, in anger, grabbed the afflicted hunter Tenn and spoke a warning to whatever might be watching through his red eyes, before unleashing Heldranc's dragonfire upon him. The chapel fell silent once more, the air thick with the metallic scent of blood and the weight of sudden loss. The surviving hunters stared in shock and grief at the companions they had just been forced to kill. Galand’s glowing eyes lingered for a moment longer before slowly fading. The party stood amid the ruins, the weight of the night’s violence hanging heavy in the twilight air of Astar’s chapel.
