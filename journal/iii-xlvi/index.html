Act III, Chapter XLVI

The storm thickened with thunder as the cursed rose again. Kojin cut one down and called for Hanto. Saoirse flew to Vaerek, who still held Filibeth, and cleared her mind; the priestess’s eyes rolled back and she slipped unconscious. He set her on a thick branch under Yuo’s watch, then returned to the field where soul-sight showed lingering cores in most of the dead save those Hanto had already claimed.

Telorin drove a spellstrike into a ghoul beside the wall of water. The thunderstorm answered with doubled lightning that arced through the barrier and flash-steamed the surrounding shallows. Silar pressed the bloated zombie with stormburst and spiritual weapon while Hanto claimed more souls. Galand, still touched by the laughter, stood unfazed as the smiling ghouls struck him. The bloated one, its eyes flaring under the tree’s tendrils, reached up and swatted Perstrin from the air.

Baiji’s blessing widened as she and the others finished the last of the smaller cursed. Yuo put a heavy shot into the big one. Vaerek dropped to heal Perstrin and shouted that they must burn the face from the tree. Wolves answered—rotting, half-skeletal things. Telorin slowed time around the trunk. Silar summoned a primal dragon in the likeness of Thalencord that rose from reddish mist and breathed fire across the face.

Filibeth stirred on her branch, looked at the grimacing visage, and knew it for Olesh; she said they must cleanse it as Thalencord once had. Vaerek centered himself and called on Heldranc. The Dragon Lord answered, took the body, and turned the wings to obsidian black. He named the face an old enemy and breathed fire laced with golden sparks. Tendrils reached for him and failed. The company pressed the wolves and the tree together. Perstrin called a ray of sunlight that briefly parted the clouds and seared the bark.

Saoirse gave Filibeth the leaf; the priestess began to commune with it while Vaerek and Heldranc, sharing the body more cleanly, hurled Valeburn into one of the face’s eyes. A guttural voice answered that it could not die. The wall of water fell. Filibeth’s light intensified and restrained the tendrils. She opened her eyes and said that he was here. A shriek answered from the storm. Golden light filled the sky.

Thalencord crested the trees, circled, and landed upon the god-tree itself. He drove his claws into the wood and pushed the fire deeper into the face until the scream ended and the visage was gone. Vaerek’s vigor returned. He flew into the remaining flame with Heldranc and drove Valeburn into the mouth while calling the Lord’s fury. The company cheered. The bodies lay still, their laughter ended and their eyes dark. The fire burned only the corruption, leaving an ordinary tree.
