Act III, Chapter XX

In the somber hours following the violent confrontation, the party and the surviving Scourgeslayers of Greymoor gathered in the chapel dedicated to Astar, the dead god of twilight. The golden radiance of Saoirse’s rituals slowly receded as she knelt near the group, checking on the survivors. The weight of the night pressed heavily; the hunters had lost three longtime companions, Greyguard Talke, Swordsman Tenn, and Manhunter Leonis, to the Red Scourge without warning.

Saoirse let out a solemn sigh and questioned the surviving hunters on why their fallen comrades had succumbed. The group stood in stunned grief. Vaerek, still smoldering from the dragonfire he had unleashed on the afflicted Tenn, looked down at the smoking corpse and stared grimly. The party debated how much to reveal about the nature of their quest. After nods of trust from Galand, Saoirse, and the others, acknowledging that the hunters had fought alongside them and lost their own, they decided the survivors deserved the truth.

Vaerek spoke carefully, starting softly: the party served a powerful draconic lord from the frozen north, one of the last Dragonlords. They sought his lost son, another wielder of draconic power, while investigating and combating the Red Scourge. He emphasized their protective role and that they meant no harm, looking the hunters earnestly in the eyes. He recounted attempting to purge the Scourge from Leonis using his vigor-healing abilities, only for the afflicted man to speak, something previously unheard of. The flames Vaerek had unleashed carried draconic essence, perhaps anathema to the Scourge.

Inside the chapel, Saoirse approached Jiatra, offering help and joining her in quiet prayer and condolences for the fallen. She assured the silent priestess that both Arleth and Sar Sareen would hear the names of their lost friends. Saoirse also examined the three statues in the altar chamber: Nadra, Goddess of Night and Moon, Astar, God of Twilight and Transformation, and Fena, Goddess of Day and Sun. She noted the symbolic gathering of celestial forces and reflected on her fallen friend Corvyr, a follower of Fena. The twilight outside and the events of the evening suggested lingering divinity.

As night deepened, preparations for rest began. Galand and Sir Brigh ventured out into the pouring rain to dig graves. The young Glaurfeldt warrior worked steadily despite the downpour. Brigh bonded with him, expressing deep gratitude for his aid in the fight and sensing a divine spark within Galand. He urged the young man to keep his heart open to whatever god called to him. Galand mentioned his past connection to Mano. Brigh shared his own history of faith and loss.

Inside, Vaerek gathered tattered cloth to serve as respectful burial shrouds. The bodies were laid out with dignity, hands clasped, eyes closed, despite the grim necessity of their earlier destruction. Saoirse left a vial of holy water wrapped in vinery on the altar as an offering - for her old friend Coryvr as much as it was also for the Gods.

Eventually, the group settled down to sleep in a drier section of the chapel, away from the bloodstained floor. In their sleep, dark dreams plagued them.

Saoirse found herself before a strange pink tree unlike those she knew. Three indistinct figures stood before it; roots spread in all directions. She felt deep worry, fear, and helplessness, as if something fundamental was wrong.

Vaerek dreamed as if he were Heldranc the Dragonlord. Fire raged everywhere and hundreds of bodies were strewn about, beneath the crimson moon. The presence of his patron overwhelmed him.

Wren walked through shadows in a dark forest. Whispers surrounded him, escalating into moaning and wailing. A solitary Godtree with silver leaves stood amid the gloom. The sound of a large bell ringing awakened him.

Telorin and Perstrin shared an identical vision: a green flame flickering in a great brazier. Beyond it, seven indistinct figures loomed. In the fire, a reflection of red eyes faded to dust.

Rallice relived the fall of his home city of Fala. Screams echoed as people disintegrated into dust around him.

Galand saw rays of light forming and then being enveloped by a crimson moon. He awoke in a stupor; Perstrin, already awake, informed him that his eyes had glowed red for a moment before fading.

The party awoke unrested. Saoirse gently roused Vaerek, noting his white-knuckled grip on his sword and warmer-than-usual breath. They shared their dreams quietly at first. Vaerek described becoming Heldranc amid fire and a crimson moon. Saoirse spoke of the pink tree and roots. Wren appeared deeply distraught, stroking his beard and staring off. Vaerek openly asked if everyone shared the experience.

As they discussed the shared visions - green braziers, silver Godtrees, crimson moons, and red eyes - Wren recounted his nightmare of whispers turning to wails, the silver Godtree, and the bell that woke him - twice, he claimed, though the ruined chapel had no functioning bell. Saoirse suggested writing the dreams down while they were fresh in their memories. Rallice provided pages from an old field notebook. The group recorded the details carefully.

Galand confided in Sir Brigh about his glowing red eyes. The paladin listened cautiously but supportively, offering to help monitor and understand the divine, and potentially ominous, spark within him. Conversations turned to possible connections between the dreams, the Red Scourge, the fading of gods, and larger forces at play. Saoirse asked Jiatra and Brigh about dispelling magic on the Scourged; attempts had limited effect, suggesting a power on par with the Fading itself. Telorin theorized the source might rival Mano’s strength. Brigh explained that the hunters only dreamt in peace since they were blessed by Sareen.

With the dead properly shrouded and graves prepared, the group offered final respects. They then set out on the road toward Greymoor, a four-day journey on foot. The hunters guided them through the rainy landscape of Yollonde.

Upon arrival, Greymoor appeared as a dilapidated but cozy village. The returning Scourgeslayers were greeted warmly by commonfolk in the streets, praised as local heroes. The party reached the Hunters’ guildhall, adorned with holy symbols of Sar Sareen. Gratitude was expressed for the shelter and aid provided in the chapel.
