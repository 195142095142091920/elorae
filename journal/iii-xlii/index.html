Act III, Chapter XLII

They set off out of Kuroishi toward an old outpost of Kaiden, spending the translation scrolls an hour at a time so that Hanto spoke and Saoirse carried his words. From the heights, the country lay open, fair, and visibly battered. Aoife flew high. The land fell sheer to the south and more gently to the north, where the rivers ran down, and Saoirse judged that a basin would lie that way. The birds had sung of a great pink tree through such a country. She believed she knew the likely direction of this land's Godtree.

Hanto knew it. It stood in the Emperor’s Wood, in Shune’s Basin, where the Lord of Kaiden had gone once a year. Only noble blood was meant to look upon the leaves. Saoirse explained that In other lands, trees of this kind had been threatened, and the Dragonlords had tried to defend them. Shune, as he told it, was a lady who had united her people against an old oppression. What Kaiden remembered was the endurance. Hanto would die with a sword in his hand.

The Remnants had been made the same way. After the fall of the empire, Hanto met Yuo, who nearly shot him, because other Kaiden soldiers had already gone mad. They found Silar, whose company had died, and in a village of survivors found Kojin, a farmer gifted with a blade, and his wife Baiji. Thalancord took them in, sent them south to gather forces, and vanished. That was eight or nine months past. His gold had been restorative: courage washing a field as light, and the madness seeming to quiet under it.

Fort Red Eve, high in the mountains of Kaiden.

That night Vaerek told Silar the rest. He explained again to the scholar that he was Heldranc’s vessel, given eyes and baptized in flame. Silar, worn thin by the work of putting innocents to the sword, asked to join the Kindred when this was done, and brightened further at the thought Pethengorom, a Dragon willing to share the deep knowledge he pursued.

The fort stood in the wind, covered in a dusting of snow. They knocked. Aoife saw no movement for a time. Soon, the great portcullis opened upon one old man with a cane. He was called Master Yudai - alone for two months, hoping stone and height would let him finish his days in quiet. A nearby village had gone to the madness, laughing, eyes orange and bleeding. His sons were long dead. He hoped to be met by Vana, goddess of death. He had not seen Thalancord, and he spoke as if that plight had already ended in disaster. He invited them in.

As they talked around a pit of flame and ash, the fire climbed. Vaerek lifted his blindfold. His soul was tattered. Tendrils of the soul extended from the core of the elder's body and yearned toward the others. Saoirse set a hand upon his head. The magic was beyond their rank, akin to what had possessed Nyralshirad, yet unalike its composition. While the Dark was cold, this was hot as a hand over a campfire. Her cleansing rebounded. She fell back into Vaerek, swearing she had been burned, and found no mark.

The pull left the man and took the room. Saoirse gazed upon the pit. Vaerek was held by the soul. Galand felt the same enamoration in the flame. Thunder cracked outside. The old man said they would be safe here, and that he joined them.

Master Yudai sits, enthralled by the flame of Izil.


Saoirse knew the flame. She had met it at the start of the journey, beside that candlemaker who walked into the desert and died. She put a gust through the pit and brought down a flash of rain indoors. The firepit died. The hearth behind them, rose higher, and Aoife shrieked at the coming storm. Vaerek drew Valeburn and set the edge at the elders throat. The old man muttered words and beckoned them to continue - the flame would devour his bones.

Vaerek's eyes changed, and the voice, and Heldranc looked at a wet room and a hearth still raging. Saoirse named the presence, this was Izil. Heldranc lowered the sword, turned to the flame, and gazed deeply upon the flame.
