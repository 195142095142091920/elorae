Act III, Chapter XXX, Dream III

The night after the duel with Vaerek in the summit, Haethlin’s voice found Galand in sleep and pulled him into lucidity. The red moon stood over a castle he had never seen - not Mano’s seat, but another hold in the Glaurfeldt, built into lush green above a river of mana. The water ran pale and sparkling, a bluish-white purple that threw mist into the wind.

Stairs climbed from the bank to a terrace of potted flowers and small falls. Mano stood with his back to view until Galand arrived, then turned and welcomed him back to the Dream.

Galand spoke in one breath: he had entered Vorn’s darkness and locked eyes with the prince on a green plain; he had watched Cadarinost die before him and another dragon nearly follow. .

Footsteps came down a double stair around a marble fountain. The lord of the castle wore polished armor that depicting a visage of skulls, and a red cloak that moved like water or blood in the wind. He held his helm at his side and showed pale skin, long black hair, and eyes that held power. They exchanged a formal greeting.

He was Vallorca, Vestige of the Formless Soul. Galand named himself, with the matching title of Vestige of the Light.

Haethlin re-entered behind another new figure. She came in plate molded to her frame, two swords, the same red cloak, and a smile that was both surprise and welcome.

Is this the boy of the light?

The three warriors of the old gods stood before their Lord. The Apostle of the Wild was yet unfound. The Elder Soul had been destroyed by Aghor long ago, and that saga involved the corruption of the Dark Soul.

Mano asked Galand to again recount his experience in the desert. Galand began with Vorn - the swelling dark, the channeled light that did not banish it, the fear he hoped had gone both ways, and then the dragonslayer. Cadarinost falling with a black-armored figure on his back, long black caped, spiked helm, a glaive that punched through dragon hide. Heldranc had named him Eig.

Mano’s calm broke. He white-knuckled the axe. Eig was no sorcerer. He knew that name, from long, long ago. The Lord High Commander of the Order of Dragonslayers of the Empire of Raim. He must have been in league with others - someone had opened that green portal for him. Galand, remembering the words of Telorin and Perstrin, said the magic had looked like Perstrin’s work in the Glaurfeldt, when they had met in the burning K'bas.

Yena tilted her head in emotion. One child of Heldranc was dead, another dismembered. Her knowledge of the Kindred seemed extensive. She asked about Darcdressil. Galand explained that they had tried that land and turned back due to the Red Scourge. Thalancord would be the next quest.

She asked whether Mano had told him of the Six Kings. The Kings had been sent to ward the surface against darkness and ready it for the Faithful. War with Dalamand drove them into the Silver Reaches - the land that would become the Ochre Depths. There they began a rite of the red moon. Sar Sareen interrupted it, not knowing fully what she would do, and its incompleteness created the Scourge: minds gone, bodies shielded against the dark. Darcdressil still lived in that mess. The Queen of Yollonde had forbidden the approach.

Galand told them where Filibeth kept her watch - the god-tree at Athag'ir in Hesk, often alone. Vallorca and Yena looked at one another. Great news, they said. The fighting could end all together with her execution.

Galand stopped them. Filibeth was not there now; she was in Nathalor. He stood with the Faithful, but he made his own choices. Filibeth had helped him, she was not yet the evil they prophesize. Vorn was the evil they could see. If there was any road on which she lived after Vorn fell, he would take it and fight anyone who barred it. If there was a road where she could be saved, he would walk it. Mano would keep an eye on that possibility, but he saw no other road. Galand said perhaps there were roads Mano could not see.

The formal meeting ended. Mano turned back to the horizon. Galand invited Haethlin into the square and asked the others whether they might meet in the waking world. The Kindred's servants were good people who did not know the full purpose; they would next seek Thalencord, a warrior whose mentor was gone.

Yena’s purpose was her lords. Her blades would end the fighting, and the fighting ended with the death of the princess. Both darkness and princess had to fall, in whatever order. Galand set his hammer between them and asked them to help him try Vorn first. Maybe the princess was not the true unraveler.

Mano turned and loomed close. He commanded the boy of the light to never disobey his command nor raise his voice. Galand bowed his head, breathed, and rose. He had followed Mano since childhood without knowing why. Now he knew. He had met her, and she seemed to have a heart. If her fate was to unravel the world, so be it - but if a sliver of another path existed, he would press it and tell them all. If the chance of execution came and no other way appeared, he would not ask them to hesitate, and he would tell his party what he had done.

Yena said they would be in contact. Vallorca shook his hand and hoped they would meet outside the Dream, perhaps to learn who was stronger. Haethlin walked Galand to the edge of sleep. The boy was young and not yet apathetic; whether his hope was naivety remained to be seen.

The Dream faded. Morning waited in Nathalor.

Act III, Chapter XXIV, Dream II

The night following their return from Yollonde, the red moon found Galand in sleep once more. The dream became solid. He stood fully present, armored, weapon at his side, the air thick with the weight of ancient places.

Haethlin’s voice greeted him first.

"Child of the Stars. Thou'rt being summoned to the Dream. May we ask of thy presence?"

Galand responded an affirmation.

"Sir Helviath. Young Knight of the Light. Thou hath been found once again and thou heed'th the call of duty. Answers and guidance, thou'rt owed. Thou asketh of our Lord Apostle. King of the Glaurfeldt, Liberator of Dathenor, Conqueror of Abor'rylith, Defender of the Silent City, Enemy of Enthar, Guardian of the Realm, and Aghor's Champion. Thy audience is granted."

Then Mano stood before him.

Tall and viciously muscular, skin like leather weathered with age, ash-haired, red-eyed, leaning upon the great ancestral axe Glaidrogh. He spoke with a deep, accented voice that carried absolute authority.

“Child of the Stars. I welcome thee to our Faithful Covenant. Cast forth thy doubts and inquiries. Thou shalt know naught but the truth.”

Galand asked the question that had weighed heaviest.

Why Filibeth? Why must she die, was there no other way?

Mano answered without flinching. Aghor Himself had granted the visions. The precise acts remained veiled, but the certainty was total. Filibeth's path, whatever form it took, would lead to the death of God and the ruin of the world. Enough that a father had sought the death of his only child. Capture would not suffice. The risk of her continued existence was too great.

Then Mano shared the visions.

As the Lord placed his hand on the knight's bowed head, Galand felt them as Mano had felt them: waves of suffering, the unraveling of order, the presence of the Creator extinguished from the world. He opened his eyes knowing, with the same bone-deep certainty, that if Filibeth were removed from the story, the ruin would not come. He also saw the genuine pain on Mano’s face. This was no madman’s joy. It was duty carried at terrible cost.

Galand accepted the blessing of Cressana.

Crimson moonlight poured down upon him. Strength bloomed in his limbs - inhuman, surging. His mind became a sealed fortress against shadow and psychic intrusion. Dark magic, elder magic, the entire framework of the void found no purchase. For a heartbeat, his eyes tried to turn red, then the fragment of Caurobor’s Light Soul burned brighter, holding them gold and fierce.

He apologized for having once believed Mano mad. Mano accepted it without bitterness. Had the events of the forest come a year later, Galand would have become a Sword of Mano, granted the same clarity that had driven Elrunal and the others.

They spoke of Heldranc. Mano learned that the dragon still lived, brooding in Nathalor. Galand urged him to offer Heldranc the truth - so the old dragon need no longer carry the weight of the grave unholy sin alone. Mano considered it in silence, the possibility of an ancient alliance rekindled.

As a gesture of trust, Mano tilted Glaidrogh toward him. Galand grasped the axe. Power flooded him - raw and ancestral. The imprisoned souls of three millennia of vanquished enemies whispering at the edge of hearing. He felt what it was to hold a weapon that had won untold wars of yore and which was able to fell a Godtree in a single stroke. Ambition and awe warred in him as he returned it.

Haethlin stepped forward and gave the briefest outline of the Disciples of the Void: their names, their domains, their hierarchy under Ito Gangara, and the dark divinities that waited beyond her. Knowledge that would have taken the party months to uncover was laid bare in moments.

At the end, Mano named him Sword of Mano - an honorary title, a mark of grace. Haethlin promised they would meet again, and that Galand would soon encounter the other children of the Ancients.

The dream faded.

Galand awoke in his bed, the weight of new strength and terrible knowledge settled deep in his bones. Soon, he would stand, readying himself for the journey to the lands of frost.

Act III, Chapter XXII, Dream I

The world dissolved.

One moment Galand sat among his companions. The next, every sense told him he stood elsewhere, fully, lucidly present. He could look down and see his own feet, his armor, the familiar weight of the greathammer. The air carried the scent of the place where he had been born. Home. The Glaurfeldt, or something that wore its shape.

A figure waited ahead.

Haethlin of the Silent City regarded him with ancient patience. The White. The Silent King. Demon of Light. He spoke with the measured cadence of one who had watched ages pass.

Galand learned of himself that he was not merely a warrior of distant lands. Within him burned a fragment of the Light Soul, the original spark of Caurobor, Ancient of the Light, one of the Titans who had shaped the world at the beginning of time. That same power was shared with other children of the Elder Gods. They were called the Faithful. They were servants of Mano, the champion of Aghor, the greatest warrior the world had ever known, the one destined to break the darkness and restore the rule of God.

Haethlin named the enemies clearly: the Disciples of the Void. Vehem Adahr, Mintas Fae, Bel Harath, Ito Gangara, and Vorn. They served the Dark Divinities and sought to reassemble the shattered Dark Soul, whose secret of reconstruction lay within the obliterated mind of the mortal who had once claimed it. That mind now whispered through every shadow and dark place in the world. Vorn listened to those whispers. Wren had begun to hear them as well. They called it the Living Dark.

The Red Scourge, Haethlin explained, was no deliberate curse of the Six Kings. It was the corrupted remnant of a failed warding ritual. The Kings were once Swords of Mano, high champions of the Glaurfeldt. Sir Amirian the Valorous, Sir Tath, the Tower Knight, Sir Imentos the White, Sir Valinvoren of the Oluine, Sir Hetraxis, the Shrine Guardian, and Sir Kaliant, the First Blade. They were sent to the realm above to instill the rule of God. They were encountered by the heathen gods, the divines of the fourth age. At the command of Mano, all but the one God were heralds of the darkness, and were ordered to be purged. After their victory against Dalamand, the Kings had attempted to shield the land against the dark through the southern Godtree. Holy Lady Sar Sareen interrupted them. In doing so, the magic was scattered, imperfect and broken. Now, when the red moon entered a dreamer’s mind, the incomplete ward ignited, protecting the soul from darkness as it stole reason.

Galand demanded answers about Mano’s war against the forest and against Filibeth. Haethlin did not deny it. The princess, they believed, would one day unravel the world. The details were lost to the murk of time, but the command had come with the clarity of God. Filibeth herself did not know the full truth.

Galand stood his ground. He would not betray his companions. He would not formally accept the blessing of the moon. But he would walk the path. He would use the power already within him against the dark. In return, he extracted a promise: Haethlin would search for Wren and shield him from the dark whispers that sought to claim his mind.

Haethlin accepted the terms. He warned Galand of Darcdressil, the Palemoon King, whose strength was great but whose enmity toward the Six Kings ran deep. The dragons could be allies, yet their rage remained uncertain. Galand was told to continue the quest for the Draconic Lords and try to use their might against the dark, but remain wary.

When the conversation ended, Haethlin raised a hand. The dream shattered.

Galand woke at the table to the alarmed voices of his companions. He had simply fallen unconscious. They asked where he had gone. He sat in silence for a long moment, the weight of everything he now knew pressing against his chest like a second armor.

He knew too much.
