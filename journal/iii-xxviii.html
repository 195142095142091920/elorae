Act III, Chapter XXVIII

Vaerek climbed the hewn stairs with four Phantoms and peered through the rubble at the cave mouth. Rain still poured. Fire still burned. Pethengorom’s flame would not be quelled — blue at the core, crystalline and sorcerous. A few dim souls lingered among the dead. The dragon himself was gone.

Vaerek told the Phantoms the truth: the mage who had saved them could take a dragon’s form. Their fear broke into awe, then hope. He called Pethengorom’s name into the storm, but was not answered.

Below, the party chose to leave the Phantoms in their sanctuary and press north. Taleera told them more of the host: empty eyes, black hoods, a vigor gone. For years they had come in small, skulking bands. Only in the past year had they marched in numbers, organized, under a storm that had raged ceaselessly.

The caverns ran deep, lined with torches. The sheer pathway dropped into blackness. They walked for a time until two dwellers stopped Montmorian and pointed at his chest. The sigil on his Fala armor was the sigil of their enemy, the Revenants.

Telorin spoke the thought no one wanted: perhaps the Revenant Host were Falans taken in the Fading, corrupted by something darker. Montmorian’s stare went distant. Saoirse turned and trudged back to the entrance to see with her own eyes.

Outside, the canyon was a sea of burning dead. Dragon-fire did not touch her. She turned bodies until she found what she feared: blackened plate that held the symbol of the Holy Three, cloaks dyed or charred over a blue that Rallice had worn. She cut a breastplate free and tore a scrap of the old color. In the thunder she paused and asked Arleth if that sea of death was what the pilgrimage had meant. Ulric’s warning returned to her: the path she walked would lead to ruin. She fled the moans of the dying back into the cave.

She put the proof in Montmorian’s hands. He stared a long time. Then he swore that whoever had done this would feel their wrath, and with a shout he hurled plate and cloak into the dark below.

They walked on along a ledge above a drop that had no bottom. Vaerek heard movement far down — too large for vermin — and then a pulse that was not natural. Galand heard it next. Darkness poured through the cavern like a tide, swallowing torchlight. Magic filled the air, high and dark, with no single source. Souls rose from the pit and reached the edge.

Montmorian drew the Sword of Torados, an ancient alabaster blade of a Val'norain that threw its own light. Vaerek’s cloak split into wings. Telorin laid haste on Galand. The host stepped out of the dark as if walking on open air.

Montmorian did not hold back. The glowing sword cut and kicked the first revenant back into the dark. Galand’s hammer answered with thunder. Vaerek struck from above. Saoirse, for the first time, went on the offensive: an icicle grown with Aszurithice’s precision, vitality sparking as it lodged in a throat. They were undead.

The fight spilled along the ledge. Revenants swung with empty, inhuman strength; void-magic slid off Galand, ineffective. Phantoms fought at their side. One of their guides fell. Saoirse put a glowing hand on the survivor’s shoulder and told him to strike for his friend.

Telorin, back to the dark, heard whispers from within. He answered with a cloud of rust. Vaerek healed, called Heldranc’s name, and rose again. An orb of light bloomed deeper in the cavern. What more waited in the dark was unknown.
