Act III, Chapter XLIII

The hearth still burned behind Master Yudai, and Heldranc looked through Vaerek into the flame with Valeburn lowered at his side. Saoirse called rain into the room; it drowned the pit fire, yet the hearth climbed higher, and she set the blindfold over Vaerek’s eyes while naming the power that held him as belonging to Izil. Hanto judged the old man free of Olesh and drew his sword, whose green enchantment yearned toward the soul already reaching for them.

Vaerek returned only partway to himself, moving enfeebled, while the heat hurt him in a way fire almost never did. Saoirse raised a wall of water across the hearth and spoke her druidic chant, and the flame fought before falling back to embers. Yudai rose then, speaking of bones and warm ash, and Hanto took his head in a single stroke. Galand watched the trail of the soul enter the blade. Vaerek drove Valeburn into what remained, and Heldranc’s deeper voice rose under his own as the hearth blew apart.

Kojin found the blood clean of 'venom'. Saoirse closed the old man’s eyes and named what they had faced: Izil, Lord of Flame, whom she and Telorin had met once in Greenbank, a power that took those who stared too long into his fire. Vaerek chose to let Heldranc stay. The lord spoke through him, confirming that the enthrallment had broken, and that Izil was a known ashen lord from beyond the Silent City. He welcomed the Thalane Remnants into Nathalor upon the completion of their quest. They spoke of the storms, of Raith, and of the old binding between Shiok and consuming flame, though Heldranc would not choose their road for them.

They settled on the Godtree. If Thalancord lived he might be waiting there, and Filibeth could begin to reclaim it; the remnants would accompany them. Before he withdrew, Heldranc answered that Izil’s flames were not born of dragons and would indeed harm them.

Vaerek set Yudai’s body in the cracked hearth and breathed dragonfire over it until only ash remained. They left at dawn Ai Ishi. Aoife led them north along the mountain road; a week passed with no word of Thalancord, and at the edge of Kaiden they crossed into Ai Ishi, where the curse had already touched the country. A wood rose ahead of them, and they turned northeast off the road into land that grew steadily lusher.

They could not find the tree. Hanto remembered that in the old days the Emperor’s Guard had entered these woods alone while the rest waited on the road, and it seemed the place was concealed as the K'bas had been. Saoirse called Filibeth through the leaf. Green mist gathered, and the princess stepped into the open light, easing the barrier of tongues so that each heard the others clearly.

She touched the river and then the leaf, and a spot of brown appeared where the water met it. Saoirse felt rot in the stream, the same signature she had known in Athag'ir, and named Horlg, divine of plague and blight. If the water could wither a leaf of a god-tree, the tree itself might already be dying. Aoife went up once more and found a gnarled, twisted tree standing dead on a riverside. They turned toward it, knowing Filibeth would need them if resistance waited there.
