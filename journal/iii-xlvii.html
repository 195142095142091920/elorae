Act III, Chapter XLVII

Thalencord’s flame had cleansed the face from the god-tree, and the company stood in the rain while the dragon rose upon a wind that quenched the last fires. Filibeth held the scarred leaf and spoke of the corruption banished; Saoirse set her hand upon the brown mark that would remain.

From behind the roots came a figure clad in gold and silver, yellow of eye and weathered by long travel, and the remnants knelt before him. Through Vaerek the voice of Heldranc answered, and the son was astonished to learn that his father shared the vessel. Olesh had been cast down once more, yet one still remained; the remnants would open a way for his return. Heldranc answered that all who willed it would be received into the halls of stone and flame. The remnants accepted the honor.

While the others spoke, Filibeth led Saoirse to the tree. Saoirse laid her hand upon a root and was filled with a rushing of visions without order. Filibeth steadied her and showed her how to anchor the flood in memory. Together they beheld the tree as it had stood whole, then the fall of the clergy, the withering, the fire of Eizel, the laughter of Shiok, and at last the mad laughter of Olesh. A hooded shape of orange flame appeared amid thunder. Filibeth drew them forth and said the tree would require days of deep communion; Saoirse answered that she would remain.

Perstrin called, and a portal of green tore the air. They passed through, Thalencord last. Pythagoram greeted him as brother and marked the missing arm; they embraced. Montmorian and Nyralshirad waited. The welcomes were warm, yet shadowed by the loss of Cadarinost. Heldranc descended and embraced his son. The remnants named themselves the Order of Thalane. He bade them retire and rest; on the morrow there would be a feast for the return of the Gold Drake.

In the chambers they bathed in the central pool. Stewards brought soaps and tea. Telorin raised his cup and said that he and Perstrin would depart. This had been their final quest. They would seek the kingdom of sorcery. Perstrin spoke of the death he had faced and of a debt of life paid on both sides. Hanto raised his glass and said their magic would be missed.

On the morrow the Kindred sat without armor in the long hall, and there was an air of family after the madness. Heldranc welcomed them as a kindred of souls bound in strength. Montmorian spoke of memory for the fallen. Nyralshirad said he owed them his life. Filibeth gave thanks for a home. Telorin said that he and Perstrin would leave after the feast to seek their own path. Glasses were raised. Heldranc lifted his last and spoke: “By the power and by the vigors.” The feast began.
