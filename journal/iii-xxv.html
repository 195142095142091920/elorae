Act III, Chapter XXV

The blizzard had quieted. In the sudden stillness Galand rolled the body over.

Rallice was dead.

Saoirse knelt beside him, one hand cradling his head, the other searching in vain for a pulse that would never return. Blackened veins traced outward from his eyes, as though he had gazed upon something so absolute that it had simply unmade him. Vaerek, still blinded, stumbled toward the sound of their voices and sank to one knee in the snow.

Aszurithice approached through the calming weather, her form once more human, pale as frost, armor trailing thin veils of cold. She looked down at the fallen knight.

“Not necromancy,” she said, voice low and ethereal. “He gazed upon the Void.”

She asked permission to guard the soul. Vaerek made space without hesitation. Aszurithice knelt, laid her hand upon Rallice’s brow, and spoke in draconic. Frost came from her fingertips, sealing the body against further decay. When she rose, the finality of it settled over them all.

Telorin’s hallucination finally fractured under the weight of contradiction. One moment he stood alone in a private void; the next the world snapped back into place and he saw them gathered around the body. He stumbled forward through the snow and sank to his knees beside the others.

They could not remain on the open ridge. Saoirse called upon Filibeth through the leaf. The portal opened. Warmth and the scent of stone replaced the howling cold of Frostfell Reach as the party stepped back into the sanctuary of Nathalor, Galand still carrying Rallice in his arms.

Filibeth met them in the throne room. The Dragonlords gathered. Montmorian arrived last, hope on his face turning to devastation the moment he saw the body.

Rage followed close behind sorrow.

He turned on Aszurithice. Why had she not answered the summons? Why had a knight of Fala died while she remained silent? Aszurithice met the accusation without flinching. She had been deep in the Underrealm, hunting an entrance to the Core, concealing herself from the scrying of Ito Gangara - the Hungerer whose spirits stole souls and bound them into a hive of the dead. The magic they wielded was of the Void itself: older than the Infinite, anathema to every living soul, capable of warping mind and destroying it outright. Only two nights earlier had she returned to the surface.

Nyralshirad delivered the second blow. Two of her brothers - Vabaldinur and Salhinurash - were dead.

Aszurithice’s glacial composure cracked for the first time.

Montmorian’s fury did not cool. He demanded purpose for the death. He demanded that no more of their servants fall while the Lords remained in hiding. Then he left the chamber, carrying Rallice’s body.

Galand had already slipped away. Somewhere deeper in the halls a great crash echoed - stone and rubble, the sound of his grief breaking stone.

Saoirse examined Vaerek’s eyes in the quiet that followed. No magic lingered to be dispelled. The blindness was physical yet unnatural: the same dark, clouded streaks that had marked Rallice’s death, only lesser. She could not promise a cure.

The party gathered in Telorin’s workshop, the forge still carrying the memory of Wren’s banter and Rallice’s bags of ingots left exactly where he had set them. Grief hung thick in the air. They turned to research instead, pouring over tomes that spoke of the Infinite and the darker framework beyond it - the Cosm, the Elder Magic of the Void. Pethengorom confirmed what they had already felt: this power did not answer to souls born of the Infinite. It corrupted. It controlled. It could not be mastered by ordinary means.

Telorin began work on a wide-brimmed hat for Perstrin, runes meant to sharpen recall and understanding of the arcane. The project was half practical, half desperate distraction.

Three days passed.

In the deep chamber of the Fallen, beneath torchlight and the glowing eyes of the Dragonlords, they laid Rallice to rest.

Montmorian spoke first. He had not known the knight long, but he had known his father, and Rallice had been a surviving sword of Fala - proof that the kingdom still lived. That sword was now sheathed. The duty would pass to those who remained.

Telorin laid a handmade crest of Fala beside the body. Saoirse spoke of integrity and the quiet strength that had marked the man. Others each said their remembrances. Filibeth placed a single leaf upon his chest. The stone lid slid into place.

Vaerek stayed longest, blind hands resting on the cool stone, unable even to look upon his friend one final time in his blindness.

Research continued. Pethengorom believed a warding against the Void might be possible for mortals. Something that shielded not body or mind, but their souls. He would speak with Heldranc. The party leaned toward the desert next, to search for Cadarinost. They would save Nybei for last.

A steward came for Vaerek. Heldranc requested his presence alone, high above, in a vast open chamber carved into the mountainside where the winter wind still howled across the threshold.

Saoirse released his hand to the steward. Vaerek went upward into the cold, leaving the others behind.
